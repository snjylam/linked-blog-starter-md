- Ensures balance in rights and obligations between insurer and consumer. 
- Protects consumers against unfair contracts, e.g.: 
	- misleading/omitted information that may influence consumer decisions at point of sale;
	- change of premium/charges/services/claims without clearly defined explanations;

	- charges for the period for cover after policy has been cancelled. 

**Equality legislation** 
- Prevent direct or indirect discrimination on the basis of a protected characteristic (e.g. gender, race, sexual orientation).
	- Direct discrimination: treat someone less favourably because of a characteristic.
	- Indirect discrimination - treat everyone in the same way but disadvantages some people because they have that characteristic (e.g. proxy factors).

<u><b>Chapter 9-10: Solvency Assessment</b></u>
**Solvency II Valuation of Assets**
- Assets are valued based on a market-consistent approach, i.e. using market value and mark-to-model (if not possible).
- Recoveries from reinsurance are shown as an asset in the balance sheet.

**Solvency II Valuation of Technical Provisions**
**Technical Provisions**
- The technical provisions are valued based on a market-consistent approach.
- The technical provisions consists of the BEL and RM. 
- The technical provisions should represent the amount the insurance company would have to pay in order to transfer its obligations immediately to another insurance company.

**Best estimate liability**
- BEL = PV(Future Benefits + Expenses - Future Premiums).
- BEL is the present value of expected future cashflows discounted using a ‘risk-free’ yield curve.
	- Future premiums are taken up to the contract boundary.
	- The risk-free yield curve:
		- is based on swap rates / government bonds with a credit risk adjustment to reflect risk of counterparty default;
		- published by EIOPA by currency;
		- can be increased by a matching adjustment with approval (if there is long-term predictable liabilities, and can hold matching assets to maturity, hence there is no exposure to risk of changing spread on the assets).
		- or volatility adjustment (if risk management requirements are met, e.g. liquidity plan and sensitivity analysis, can apply the adjustment to reduce the risk of forced asset sales in the event of extreme bond movements).
	- The assumptions are best estimate with no prudential margins.
	- The projections should allow for all expected decrement.
	- The projections should be on policy-by-policy basis (approximations and model points allowed provided certain conditions are met, including validation of accuracy).
	- The contract boundary is the point at which a company can unilaterally terminate the contract, refuse to accept a premium or change the premiums or benefits in such a way that they fully reflect the risks). 
	- The allowance for future expenses needs to consider both overheads and directly attributable expenses, and future expense inflation.
	- No closure reserve is required.
	- Constitutes fulfilment cash flows in IFRS 17.
- **For with-profits**
	- BEL is split into guaranteed benefits and discretionary benefits. 
	- For guaranteed benefits:
		- Can start with the asset share;
		- Need to allow for any expected distribution from the estate (in line with regulations).
		- Need to reflect cost of guarantees (e.g. prospective market-consistent cost of death/maturity benefits in excess of asset share);
	- For discretionary benefits:
		- Need to use stochastic calculation. 
		- Need to relate to normal expected bonus distributions. 
		- Should not include the distribution of the estate unless a formal distribution has been approved by the regulator.
		- Need to reflect management actions.
		- Need to reflect policyholder behaviour.
		- Need to allow for smoothing.
	- The BEL cannot include value of shareholder transfers in respect of future bonus declarations.
- **For unit-linked**
	- BEL is split into unit-liability and non-unit liability. 
	- The unit liability would be the market value of the units at the valuation date. 
	- The non-unit liability would be a prospective stochastic valuation of future benefits and expenses less charges.
- **Negative reserves**
	- Reserves will be negative if PV future premiums exceeds PV (future benefits + expenses).
	- It is possible for BEL to be negative for if the best estimate of the value of future premiums exceeds the value of future benefits and expenses.
	- This will usually be the case for profitable regular premium business that has recently been written.

**Risk margin**
- The risk margin represents the theoretical compensation for the risk of future experience being worse than best estimate assumptions and for the cost of holding regulatory capital against this.
- The risk margin is determined using the ‘cost of capital’ method, i.e. based on the cost of holding capital to support those risks that cannot be hedged.
	- Insurance risk
	- Reinsurance credit risk
	- Operational risk
	- Residual market risk
- **Cost of capital method**
	- The risk margin calculation requires a projection of the future capital that the company is required to hold at the end of each projection period (e.g. year) during the run-off of the existing business.
	- The projected capital requirement is a subset of the SCR, consisting of those risks that cannot be hedged in financial markets.
	- These projected capital amounts are then multiplied by a cost of capital rate.
	- For Solvency II this is a fixed rate of 6% pa.
	- This rate can be considered to represent the cost of raising incremental capital in excess of the risk-free rate, or alternatively it represents the frictional cost to the company of locking in this capital to earn a risk-free rate rather than being able to invest it freely for higher reward. 
	- The product of the cost of capital rate and the capital requirement at each future projection point is then discounted, using risk-free discount rates, to give the overall risk margin. 
- **Simplified approach**
	- Since the projection of the SCR is potentially complex, various simplified approaches can be used.
	- For example, this could involve selecting a driver (e.g. reserves, sum at risk, policy count) which has an approximately linear relationship with the required capital or its components. 
	- The initial capital requirement can be expressed as a percentage of that driver, and the projected capital is then approximated as the same percentage of the projected values of the driver. 
	- In practice, more sophisticated methods using a combination of drivers and correlations may have to be used.

**Solvency II Solvency Capital Requirements**
**Standard Formula**
- The standard formula is based on a one-year 99.5% Value-at-Risk measure.
- The SCR is calculated separately for each individual risk within a module, by stressing the net asset value and comparing this with the unstressed net asset value.
- These values are aggregated within each module using a specified correlation matrix.
- The results for each risk module are then aggregated using another specified correlation matrix, giving the Basic SCR.
	- market risk
	- counterparty default risk
	- insurance risk
	- intangible assets
- The overall SCR is then obtained by including allowances for:
	- the operational risk module; which is calculated based on percentages of earned premiums and technical provisions.
	- the loss absorbing capacity of technical provisions; which could include the ability to reduce discretionary benefits under stressed conditions.
	- the deferred taxes.
**Internal Model**
- An internal model allows the company to tailor the solvency capital assessment to its own risk profile.
- An internal model must pass six tests before it can gain approval by the local regulator: ·
	- use test to show that the model is widely used within the company for decision-making and other processes;
	- statistical quality standards to show that minimum standards are met for assumption setting and data;
	- calibration standards to show that the assessment is equivalent to a 99.5% confidence one-year Value-at-Risk;
	- profit and loss attribution to show how the categorisation of risk chosen is used to explain the sources of profit and loss;
	- validation standards to show that the model has been fully validated and regularly reviewed;
	- documentation standards.
- Rather than using stress tests combined with correlation matrices, stochastic simulations could be used – perhaps using copulas to model dependencies between risks.
- An internal model can allow for the impact of dynamic hedging.
- A partial internal model may be used, with the standard formula being used for some risks (e.g. operational risk).

**Supervisory Intervention Levels**
- The SCR is a high-level, risk-based target (99.5% confidence) ensuring survival, while the MCR is a lower absolute floor, representing the threshold for urgent regulatory intervention, typically set between 25% and 45% of the SCR
- The MCR must be calculated and reported to the regulator quarterly.
- There is an absolute minimum capital requirement of €3.7m for life insurance companies.
- Restrictions are placed on the quality of capital that can be used to cover the MCR and SCR. 
- For example:
	- the SCR must be backed by at least 50% Tier 1 capital and less than 15% Tier 3
	- the MCR must be backed by at least 80% Tier 1 capital and no Tier 3 capital.

<u><b>Chapter 15: Analysis of surplus</b></u>
**Sources of surplus**
- Possible components of an analysis of surplus (where surplus is defined as assets in excess of liabilities) are:
	- opening adjustments – including corrections, changes to model point groupings, methodology changes
	- return on opening surplus
	- economic variances – including those on investment returns, inflation and exchange rates
	- changes in economic assumptions 
	- non-economic variances
	- changes in non-economic assumptions 
	- new business
	- other variances
	- unexplained movements.


Quantifying the surplus from each source ·
· ·
A typical process is based on rolling forward the balance sheet from the previous valuation date (both assets and liabilities) to produce ‘expected’ balance sheets.
These should allow in turn for corrections, assumption changes, changes to economic conditions, new business and experience variances.
The actual and expected balance sheets can then be compared, the difference being the ‘unexplained’

**IFRS 17 Valuation**
- GMM is used for traditional long-term life insurance business, e.g. term assurance & without profits annuities. 
- GMM discount rate is derived from observable market data. 
- VFA is used when significant proportion of cashflows vary in line with a clearly defined pool of assets, unit-linked; or has discretionary participating features, with-profits.
- VFA discount rate is derived from the pool of assets (e.g. unit / with-profits fund). 
- The company will hold:
	- best estimate liabilities (BEL);
	- risk adjustment (RA);
	- contractual service margin (CSM) if profitable, loss component (LC) if 'loss-making'.
- The BEL and RA is calculated in a similar way as Solvency II BEL and RM.
- The CSM eliminates day one profit (premium less expenses, BEL, RA). 
- The CSM offsets change in BEL and RA due to assumption changes.
- For VFA, the CSM is unlocked when the discount rate changes.
