<u><b>Q1(i): List the four most material market and life underwriting risks to the company</u></b>
**Market risks**
 - Equity risk
 - Property risk
 - Interest rate risk
 - Credit spread risk

**Life underwriting risk**
- Mortality risk
- Longevity risk
- Lapse risk
- Expense risk

<u><b>Q1(ii): Propose with reasons which direction the risks are likely to be stressed.</u></b>
**Equities and property markets**
- The adverse direction is down for equity markets;
- and property markets.
- This reduces the value of assets backing own funds;
- and the future fund-based charges on the UL business.

**Interest rates**
- The adverse direction is up for interest rates.
- This reduces the value of fixed-interest bonds backing own funds;
- and the value of assets backing annuity business;
- by more than the reduction in BEL;
- because the bonds have longer duration than the BEL.
- The non-unit BEL for the UL business is negative. If interest rates increase, this non-unit BEL would be discounted at a higher rate resulting in an increase in total BEL.
- The volatility adjustment would not change under the standard formula interest rate stress.

**Credit spread**
- The adverse direction is widening in credit spread.
- This reduces the value of corporate bonds backing own funds.
- This also reduces the value of corporate bonds backing annuity business
- and this would not be offset by a change in the volatility adjustment.

**Mortality / longevity**
- The adverse direction is up for mortality on the term assurance business due to higher claims;
- and also up on the UL business due to loss of future fund charges.
- The adverse direction is up for longevity on the annuity business;
- due to having to pay out more annuity benefits and related expenses.

**Lapses**
- The adverse direction is up for the UL business;
- due to the loss of future fund charges.
- The adverse direction is up for the term assurance business at early durations;
- as the BEL is likely to be negative at this point.
- The adverse direction is down for the term assurance business at later durations;
- as there will be a release of the positive reserves held, since no surrender values would be paid.
- There is no lapse risk for the annuity business.

**Expenses**
- The adverse direction is up;
- as this will reduce future profits.
- There may be some offset if charges are reviewable and so can be increased;
- but it may not be possible to allow for this in the standard formula SCR calculation.

<u><b>Q1(iii): Determine how the magnitude of the undiversified SCRs might have changed.</u></b>
**Equity market**
- The fall in equity values meant that the equity risk SCR component was lower at that point, before the switch into greater equity exposure and market recovery took place.
- The company has invested more into equities over the year, increasing equity market exposure.
- Hence the equity risk SCR component will have increased.

**Property risk**
- The property values had fallen over the year, reducing property market exposure.
- Hence the property risk SCR component will have decreased.

**Interest rate** 
- Since interest rates have fallen to very low levels, 
- the value of fixed-interest bonds will have increased.
- Hence the interest rate risk SCR component will have increased.

**Credit spread**
- The value of corporate bonds will be higher due to lower interest rates, so credit spread risk SCR component will have increased.

**Mortality / longevity**
- The significant number of deaths suggests that there are fewer in-force annuities at year end.
- Hence the longevity risk SCR component will have reduced.
- There may also be fewer term assurance policies in place.
- Hence the mortality risk SCR component will have reduced.
- There may be offset to the reduction in mortality / longevity risk SCR components;
- due to the lower interest rates, which will have led to a reduction in the discount rate used for BEL in the SCR calculation.

**Lapses**
- The significant number of deaths suggests that there fewer in-force unit-linked policies at year end.
- Hence the lapse risk SCR component will have reduced.
- The term assurance will likely have limited impact on the lapse risk SCR component.

**Expenses**
- The per policy expense assumption in the BEL might have increased
- due to the low volumes of business sold during the year.
- Hence the expense risk SCR component will have increased.
- There may be additional expenses incurred such as rectifying the unit pricing error, which if treated as a one-off, will have no impact on the SCR calculation.

<u><b>Q1(iv): Suggest possible reasons for a regulatory capital add-on.</u></b>
**Standard formula**
- The regulator may have felt that the company's risk profile is unlike what a standard insurer would have, which the standard formula is for;
- i.e. in terms of assets that are held;
- and lives that are insured.
- The standard formula might not adequately capture the latest view of tail risks.
- The operational risk component might not adequately capture the risk relating to unit pricing errors.
- The standard formula stresses might not reflect the potential impact of the pandemic e.g.:
	- higher deaths as disease spreads
	- higher deaths due to complications from the disease
	- greater longevity for those who survive the disease
	- if those in less good health are more likely to have died from it
	- higher lapses on unit-linked business
	- due to ongoing concerns of the economy
	- lower lapses on term assurance business
	- due to individuals placing a greater value on death benefit provided.

**Other concerns**
- The regulator may feel that the company's ORSA is poor.
- The regulator may not have confidence in the company's risk governance;
- e.g. it failed to prevent the unit pricing error.
- The regulator may not have confidence in the company's risk management processes;
- particularly given that there appear to be limited controls in place.
- Given an operational risk event has happened during the year
- the regulator might wish to see a more detailed consideration of operational risk.
- The regulator may have concerns on data quality.
- The regulator may have concerns on viability of the company given that new business has fallen very low.
- The regulator may want to ensure the capital levels of the company are in line with other comparable firms.

**Wider insurance market concerns**
- The regulator may impose an industry-wide add on as a result of changes impacting all insurers
- particularly in light of the uncertainty around the pandemic.

<u><b>Q1(v): Discuss the challenges when calibrating an internal model.</u></b>
- The company needs to meet calibration standards test set by regulator to gain approval for internal model.
- Obtaining sufficient historical data on extreme events, to calibrate the stress tests, is likely challenging.
- It will difficult to know how to use the recent data on the impact of the pandemic;
- such as the high number of deaths.
- Setting dependencies between risks could also be difficult;
- particularly given that recent experience may well differ from historical experience. 
- A significant degree of expert judgement will be required to perform the calibrations;
- and the company may not have sufficient internal expertise or may find it difficult or expensive to obtain.
- There may be challenges relating to the quality of data used for the calibration. 
- Historical data may not be relevant or credible for future use.
- The company will need to decide the extent to which the recent pandemic experience could be considered to be ‘extreme’.
- Specific areas that might be challenging include:
	- allowing for recent lapse experience 
	- allowing for recent investment market experience allowing for recent credit spread / default experience
	- assessing any selective links between recent mortality spikes and lapses
	- assessing the correlations between the various market risks during a period of economic disruption
	- assessing the correlations between market risks and lapses during the pandemic.
- The company might not be able to rely on industry consensus stresses and correlations that have emerged, as these might not be relevant to its own risk profile.
