<u><b>Q1(i): Set out how a life insurance company determines whether it should award the same rates of reversionary bonus to all types of with-profits contracts.</b></u>
- It should carry out a gross premium valuation of the with-profits contracts;
- at the current reversionary bonus rates;
- and an assumed terminal bonus rate;
- choosing specimen contracts that model the existing with-profits business;
- and compare the gross premium valuation with the earned asset share.
- It may be different classes have differences between the gross premium valuation and earned asset share  in which case different reversionary bonus rates may be necessary.
- It should consider TCF and PRE.
- It should consider past practice.
- It should consider what has been communicated to policyholders;
- e.g. PPFM.
- It should ensure the rates are supportable under various stresses.
- It should ensure the rates are supportable under new businesses.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Discuss the difficulties the company may face with paying the same reversionary bonus rates and terminal bonus rates for business in the merged fund.</b></u>
- Asset shares may have accumulated differently in the two funds.
- Asset shares at the time of the merger for similar policies could be different between;
- making it difficult to find bonus rates that are fair to both sets of policyholders;
- e.g. similar by size or premium or date of entry.
- This could be due to different investment returns;
- due to different equity backing ratios;
- or different types of assets within the two funds.
- The expenses allocated to asset shares could be different;
- for both initial and renewal expenses;
- and commission expense;
- and development costs.
- There charges to the asset shares could be different;
- e.g. charges for guarantees;
- or charges for smoothing;
- or charges for cost if capital.
- The surrender profits or losses allocated to asset shares may be different;
- similarly profits/losses on death;
- similarly smoothing profits or losses on maturities could be different;
- or allocated to the estate for one and not the other.
- The deduction for shareholder transfers may differ.
- The tax rates may be different.
- There may have been distributions made from the estate to policyholders in one fund and not the other.

- Final bonus rates will depend on the amount of surplus already granted via regular bonus.
- Historic bonuses could be different between the two funds.
- Since bonuses usually take account of the relationship between asset shares and;
- existing guarantees, this could make it hard to find a fair bonus rate scale;
- e.g. the split between RB and TB could be different.
- One could have lower RB than the other.
- One fund may have allocated a special bonus. 
- One might allocate compound reversionary bonus and the other simple.
- Could be a different methodology to allocating.
- One might allocate terminal bonus on sum assured and attaching reversionary bonus and the other may allocate just on sum assured or just on reversionary bonus.
- So that the asset share to guarantee ratio is very different between the funds;
- making it hard to target asset shares with the same future bonuses.

**Other considerations**
- Policyholders could have expectations based on historic bonus rates so any significant change to bonuses may be against PRE.
- Products could have been priced differently;
- for example, the guaranteed sum assured for a similar premium/age/term could be different between the two funds;
- so, a policy with a lower starting sum assured may expect higher RB than otherwise.
- Both funds may be at a different place in the smoothing cycle;
- for example, if one fund is currently paying less than asset shares it would be aiming to increase bonuses and vice versa;
- Systems may not be able to cope with the changes;
- so may need significant changes.
- Customers may complain;
- putting a strain on resources;
- and costing more.
- Customer literature may state how bonuses are calculated;
- or how asset shares are calculated;
- e.g. PPFM in the UK.
- May need to deal with bad reputation.
- Take account of TCF;
- and policyholder expectations;
- and a need to be competitive.
- May have to convince stakeholders it is fair, e.g. If there is a WPA.

<u><b>Q1(iii): Discuss other factors that Company X should consider, from a policyholder perspective, when deciding how to merge Funds A and B.</b></u>
- Whether there is already a scheme in place which places restrictions on such mergers.
- Costs involved and how;
- and who pays for the costs.
- Whether the security for any set of with-profits policyholders is reduced due to the merger.
- Similarly, the security of the with-out profits policyholders must be considered;
- both on day-1 and projected into the future;
- under different scenarios;
- and how to compensate for any material difference.
- Whether projected asset shares are likely to be lower for any policies due to: 
	- changes to the investment mix
	- changes to expenses
	- changes to charges allocated for guarantees/smoothing/capital
	- and allocation of without-profits profits or losses to asset shares.
- Similarly, will some policyholders have higher projected asset shares;
- due to reasons other than cost efficiencies.
- Whether any potential distributions from the estate would be different post-merger;
- especially if the company stops selling new business.
- The current relative value of the estates would be the key consideration.
- The company may have to consider a one-off distribution in order to make the estates consistent;
- but this could make harmonising bonus rates even harder.
- Policyholder Reasonable Expectations must be considered;
- as well as any existing literature policyholders have been given;
- and past experience;
- and TCF.
- Consider the communications necessary for policyholders.
- Service levels should remain the same.
- Changes to the smoothing policy should be considered.
- Surrender values should be no worse off.
- Consider regulation/industry practice.
- Split of future RB/TB should be considered.
- Ensure changes made are not too complex for customers to understand.

<u><b>Q1(iv): Discuss the factors that Company X should consider in deciding whether to covert the with-profits policies to without-profits.</b></u>
**Change in risk following the conversion**
- Who takes the risk and holds the capital? 
- Likely to be shareholders else cannot distribute the estate fully;
- or perhaps transfer to the with-profits fund;
- but compensation for the cost of capital will still need to be factored in.
- In either case there needs to be a fair charge for the capital. 
- There may be a conflict of interest between Shareholders who take the risk and policyholders who pay the charge. 
- Any remaining estate will need to be distributed fairly.
- Since there is a run-off plan, any distribution needs to be consistent with this plan.
- The run of plan will have set expectations;
- but may not state what to do when converting to non-profit;
- so, judgements and assumptions will have to be made to ensure a fair distribution.
- The run-off plan distributes annually by size of estate, then expected outstanding duration may need to be taken into account. 
- The run off of the capital requirements and so the estate may have to be considered. 
- However, this approach could replicate the tontine which is what the company wants to avoid.

**Terms for the conversion** 
- Assumptions about the value of the converted non-profit policies will have to be made.
- For example, which assets will be invested in;
- and what these assets will earn;
- or might use the risk free rate which may give less to policyholders;
- as it will be a low discount rate.
- Expected decrements will also be required;
- as well as the expected future expenses.
- If per policy expenses are expected to increase as the business drops the company may find that it is not possible to convert on reasonable terms;
- so it may have to consider selling;
- or placing into one of its existing funds.
- Unitised policyholders may prefer to have a unit-linked policy to conventional since it is closer to the current unitised contract.
- Is there any literature (e.g. scheme setting out what to do?) 
- How have other companies done this? 
- Fair treatment/policyholders’ expectations/communications/PPFM.
- Legal process may need to go through for a Scheme of Arrangement.
- Level of uplift/compensation to PHDRs for conversion.
- Views of the WPA or WPC.
- and the future profits which may have arisen on these PHDRs and impacted bonuses?
- Systems/admin implications.
- Views of regulator/tax regulator.
- Timescales to implement.
- Cost of implementing.
- Alternatives to conversion may prove better.
- What is the impact on profit profile.
