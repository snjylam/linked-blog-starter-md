<u><b>Q2(iv): Describe the actions that the company may consider to manage its with-profits business in response to fall in equity values.</u></b>
**Investment strategy**
- The company might change its investment strategy.
- If it is concerned about its financial strength
- then it might have to sell equities.
- If the company’s investment strategy involves having a defined equity backing ratio for asset shares;
- then it would have to buy equities after the market fall in order to maintain that ratio.
- If it has adopted a hedging strategy to back the cost of guarantees, e.g. using derivatives, then it may need to rebalance these hedges.

**Bonuses and payouts**
- The company is likely to reduce terminal bonus rates;
- subject to its approach to smoothing payouts.
- The extent to which terminal bonus rates need to be reduced depends on where payouts were relative to asset shares before the fall;
- due to past smoothing constraints;
- so it may be payouts are now in line with asset shares after fall, hence no need to change terminal bonus.
- The company might reduce regular bonus rates;
- in order to reduce the rate of accumulation of guaranteed benefits.
- Reducing regular bonus rates is unlikely to have a significant impact in the short-term.
- The company might review its approach to surrender values / pay less on surrender.
- e.g. bring surrender values into line with the lower unsmoothed asset share.
- Surrender values are more likely to be reduced significantly than contractual payouts such as death or maturity benefits.
- It might introduce market value reductions (MVRs) on the unitised with-profits business;
- in order to protect remaining policyholders;
- from surrendering policyholders receiving significantly more than asset share.
- If the company is currently distributing the estate, it may consider whether this needs to be reviewed;
- to ensure that the approach remains fair;
- to both remaining and exiting policyholders.

**Other actions**
- The company might increase any variable charges on unitised with-profits business in order to ensure that expenses continue to be covered.
- The company may increase its monitoring of management information on leavers.
- This will be an important input to deciding which action to take and how quickly
- for example: the higher the number of exits, the greater the pressure to act.
- The company might withhold shareholder transfers from being taken out of the fund.

**General considerations**
- Any action taken needs to be consistent with PRE,
- any treating customers fairly requirements
- and policyholder documentation / communications
- such as the PPFM or equivalent.
- Before taking action, the company might decide to wait to see whether the stock market quickly recovers.
- The company is likely to consider the size of the estate
- as this might influence the speed at which any actions are taken.
- The company needs to consider what actions have been documented in any run-off plan that might be in place for the conventional with-profits portfolio;
- and any relevant court scheme that might apply.
- The company should consider what actions it took in similar situations in the past.
- The company will have to follow its existing governance arrangements;
- including the authority needed to take any of the actions, such as:
    - the Board
    - the With-Profits Actuary (or equivalent)
    - any With-Profits Committee (or equivalent).

<u><b>Q2(v): Discuss the possible reasons to not introduce market value reductions.</u></b>
**Backing assets**
- The equity backing ratio might be very low.
- A fall in the value of local equities may not have resulted in a material fall in asset shares.
- The value of other assets used to back asset shares (e.g. fixed-interest bonds) might have increased;
- offsetting the equity value reduction.
- Asset shares might not be invested in ‘local’ equities;
- and global equity markets might not have fallen to the same extent.
- The market might be expected to recover very quickly.

**Payouts**
- Terminal bonus rates might have been cut in response to the equity market fall;
- and this has brought payouts into line with asset shares;
- after allowing for smoothing;
- so there is no need for any MVRs.
- Similarly, payouts might have been lower than asset share before the market fall;
- e.g. due to downward smoothing;
- and they are now in line with asset shares.

**Materiality**
- The volume of unitised with-profits business might be relatively low.
- The free estate, which can absorb the extra cost, might be very large.
- If the estate is large, payouts exceeding asset shares is unlikely to have a material impact;
- on the benefit expectations of policyholders who do not surrender;
- or on the security of those benefits.
- The surrender rate might have been very low since the market fall and is expected to remain that way.
- If the surrender rate is very low, total surrender value payouts in excess of asset share might not be material;
- in terms of impact on remaining policyholders;
- and the cost could simply be absorbed through the usual smoothing or pooling process.
- However, an MVR might be introduced at a later date if surrenders rise and/or the poor equity market conditions continue.

**Constraints**
- Imposing MVRs might go against PRE, for example if they had not been applied following a previous similar market fall.
- Legislation / regulation might restrict the use of MVRs.
- The company might wish to avoid negative publicity;
- particularly if competitors are not applying MVRs.
- Terms and conditions might not permit the use of an MVR.
- MVRs cannot be imposed where there is a no-MVR guarantee in place;
- and there may be no-MVR guarantees for a significant proportion of the unitised with-profits business.
- If the no-MVR guarantee only applies at a particular policy duration, the company might expect policyholders to defer surrendering until that point.
- Systems might not be set up to be able to process MVRs.
