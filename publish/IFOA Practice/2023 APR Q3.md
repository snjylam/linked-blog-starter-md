<u><b>Q3(i): Discuss the additional reputational risks associated with this investment fund.</b></u>
**Investment fund**
- There is risk that investments do not comply with the ESG principles.
- There is a risk of poor investment performance (e.g. due to high level of defaults);
- and this is a particular risk due to the assets being loans to new start-up companies.
- There may be reputational issues relating to the lack of liquidity. 
- If green energy companies were using significant fossil fuels in the development of equipment, this has the potential to harm the company's reputation.
- The investments need to be monitored in order to ensure that they remain ESG compliant, to reduce the risk of future reputational damage.
- The launch of the new fund might leave the company open to more complaints;
- e.g. that the investment information provided was misleading. 
- The fund management charge might have to be higher for this fund/ may need to be increased in future.
- The company may not have any expertise in sustainable investments, increasing the risk of errors.
- The above could cause reputational damage;
- which could lead to higher lapses;
- and lower new business volumes. 
- However, the launching fund the fund could overall be positive for the company's reputation.

**Wider company considerations**
- Given that the target market is likely to comprise socially responsible customers, the company must also show itself to be responsible in other areas;
- e.g. diversity of staff/board members.
- The company may need to take extra measures in order to achieve ESG compliance.

<u><b>Q3(ii): Describe how the new asset class may impact each of the key types of risks.</b></u>
**Market risk**
- The company will be exposed to additional currency risk, if the industries operate in other countries.
- There should be no other material market risks;
- including credit spread widening risk;
- since the assets are fixed interest loans;
- and are held to maturity.
- There is no interest rate risk, except in the unlikely event that they can find a buyer for the asset. 

**Insurance risk**
- The company will be exposed to additional insurance risk;
- in relation to persistency;
- since early surrenders will mean that there has not been a chance to recover initial/ development expenses.
- There may be additional expense risk;
- for example, due to sales volumes being lower than expected/having a smaller average fund size than expected.
- These would result in higher per-policy expenses than expected and an inability to make expected contribution to overheads

**Credit risk**
- The company will be exposed to additional credit/counterparty risk;
- in respect of the loans issued;
- in terms of the counterparty failing to meet its obligations.
- This risk might be significant, due to the loans being made to start-up companies.
- The credit risk will largely be passed on to the policyholders;
- but the company will be impacted to the extent to which charges are expressed as a percentage of the fund value (e.g. fund management charges).
- The company is still exposed to credit risk on the loans it holds itself (e.g. where customers have surrendered), rather than those backing unit funds.
- There is a risk the underlying assets become worthless. 
- These are new ventures so there may be little data on which to measure credit/default risk.

**Liquidity risk**
- The company is exposed to additional liquidity risk;
- given that there is no liquid market for the underlying assets.
- This may cause liquidity issues if policyholders surrender;
- as the value of the bonds cannot be easily realised until the maturity date;
- and the company is left with an illiquid asset on its balance sheet, which it cannot sell.
- The company may not be able to 'sell' all of the bonds it has invested in to policyholders via the unit-linked fund, and so would have direct exposure itself to the liquidity risk of those loans.

**Operational risk**
- There is likely to be additional operational risk;
- such as mis-selling risk;
- e.g. if the customer did not realise the potential issues relating to surrendering before the loans reach full term.
- This relates to the potential liquidity issues and the possibility of a surrender payment being deferred.
- There might be additional model risk;
- in relation to systems and model changes to reflect the new assets;
- including the use of inadequate models when valuing the assets;
- and inappropriate application of any expert judgement.
- There may be some additional unit pricing risk;
- due to the assets being hard to value.
- It is unlikely there will be any change in group risk.

**Climate risk**
- Climate risk might increase
- For example, physical risks from climate change might increase;
- e.g. due to changes in the volume of water supporting the hydro-electric power generation;
- or flood risk to the hydro-electric plants
- or due to changes in climate in the countries producing fair trade cocoa and coffee;
- wildfires or disease affecting cocoa and coffee;
- leading to reduction in the value of assets. 
- Transition risks might also change;
- for example, due to regulatory/tax changes.

<u><b>Q3(iii): Discuss the profitability and marketability factors the company would take into account when determining the AMC.</b></u>
**Profitability**
- The annual management charge (AMC) needs to be sufficient to cover expected expenses ;
- and to meet the specified target profit criterion.
- The profit criterion could be stated in terms of net present value, internal rate of return, discounted payback period, or any combination of these.
- There may be a different target profit criterion for this investment fund;
- due to its sustainability / ESG nature.
- Policyholders may be willing to pay higher charges for investing in ESG;
- because they value the contribution made to the environment.
- However, it is unlikely that they would be willing to pay a higher charge for the ESG fund if this were simply to generate greater company profits.
- Shareholders may be willing to accept a lower target profit for this new investment fund.
- The company might decide to accept a lower level of profit, at least initially, to ensure that the investment fund is sufficiently attractive to generate a successful launch. 
- It could even be offered as a loss leader.
- The company needs to decide the extent to which any contribution to overheads will be loaded into the AMC.
- The company needs to consider the basis used for reporting profits.
- If this is different from the pricing basis, then it needs to understand how business decisions will impact the timing of profit emergence.

**Marketability**
- The company should perform market research to assess the size of the potential market.
- This is a niche product and customers may be uncertain about the likely expected returns.
- The company should consider the level of AMC that it is charging on its other funds;
- and the level of competitors' charges for similar funds.
- The company should consider whether the charge should be reviewable or not.
- A fixed/guaranteed AMC would be more attractive than one that is reviewable/variable.
- Customers might be wary of the risks involved in the new investment fund.
- However, ESG is a topical issue so there may be a large potential market. 

<u><b>Q3(iv): Describe the considerations to take to ensure customers are treated fairly when investing in this fund.</b></u>
- The company should adhere to any relevant regulations on TCF.
- The company should check that charges are reasonable compared with other investment/competitor funds; 
- particularly if they are reviewable;
- that they reflect a fair assessment of the costs and risks;
- be clearly disclosed and understood.
- The company needs to make clear to customers the different types of risks in relation to the investment fund.
- The company needs to ensure its sales literature is clear, accurate, and updated;
- particularly in relation to the liquidity risks. 
- The company needs to ensure the sustainability criteria is indeed met by the companies the fund is investing. 
- The company needs to ensure it does not mislead customers into thinking other investment funds are also ESG funds.
- The policy projections need to take into account specific risks and likely returns. 
- The policy documents need to clearly show how asset values are determined. 
- The company should perform due diligence on the loans.
- The company should monitor the companies in which the fund is invested and move out any investments which no longer meet the sustainability criteria. 
- There should be an appropriate level of customer service / complaint process in place.
- There should be special considerations for vulnerable customers.
- There should not be unreasonable barriers to exit.
- The discretion applied for setting early surrender values need to be communicated carefully.
