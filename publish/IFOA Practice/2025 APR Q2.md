<u><b>Q2(i): Discuss the reasons why the company should produce an ORSA prior to writing new bulk annuity business.</b></u>
- A firm is required to identify all risks it may face;
- including future risks.
- So will be relevant to a new firm to demonstrate an understanding of the risk they may face;
- and how they would mitigate these;
- our put processes/controls in place to reduce the exposure to the risk.
- Provides the regulator with confidence that mgmt. understand the future business.
- Also demonstrates that the company can meet the MCR/SCR over the business planning horizon;
- which is key for a new company;
- as may not have access to spare assets.
- Again regulator will want to be confident that the company will remain solvent in the future;
- on a range of sensitivities/scenarios.
- ORSA should be used by mgmt. when making strategic decisions;
- which is key for a new company.
- It is a requirement to produce an ORSA both prior to writing business and once it has written business.
- Bulk annuity business is likely to be very capital intensive and individual bulk annuity deals can require significant amounts of capital when they are written so it is important that mgmt. consider this when assessing its new business plans. 
- Helps to confirm whether the company should be SF or IM.

<u><b>Q2(ii): Outline the potential risk events that could arise for each of the above risks.</b></u>
**Market Risk**
- Insufficient assets in the market to match expected annuity cashflows.
- Inappropriate ALM performed;
- such that assets purchased don't match the expected annuity cashflows.
- Interest rates not at level expected.

**Mispricing risk related to insurance risk**
- Mispricing by using inappropriate longevity assumption.
- Not having sufficient data to price correctly Incorrect/insufficient member data.
- Or misinterpretation of the data it has received;
- for example with respect to spouses data;
- or spouses data;
- or benefit/scheme details.
- Wrong expenses assumed.

**Operational Risk**
- Not setting up systems to deal with expected business volumes/system failure Not hiring people with right skills.
- Or not able to hire people at the right time.
- Not choosing an outsourcer that has the necessary skills.
- Scheme data transferred incorrectly.

**Regulatory compliance risk**
- Not meeting current regulation due to lack of knowledge/skills.
- Or not meeting future changes in regulation.
- Capital add on by regulator due to concerns over risk management.

- Use consultants to help with setting up systems.

- Closely monitor regulation changes by signing up to email alerts.
- Work closely with the regulatory supervisors to avoid any late surprises.
- Establish a compliance function to monitor compliance.

<u><b>Q2(iv): Suggest possible actions the company could take to reduce exposure to the risk events.</b></u>
**Longevity increase combined with higher new business volumes** 
- No impact on assets from longevity increasing;
- but assets will increase due to higher business volumes.
- Liabilities will increase with an increase in longevity;
- as annuitants are expected to live longer.
- Liabilities will also increase if more business is written than originally expected.
- SCR will increase if longevity is strengthened;
- as stresses will be applied to higher liabilities.
- Similarly SCR will increase if more business is written.
- Overall SCR coverage is expected to reduce largely due to the longevity improvements.

**Admin outsourcer default combined with higher internal expenses for non- admin functions** 
- No impact on assets from outsourcer defaulting or;
- from higher internal expenses.
- Liabilities will increase as expected future expenses will be higher;
- due to no longer having an outsourcer;
- as have to find a new outsourcer at short notice with potentially higher costs. 
- Also, internal costs are higher than expected 
- so modelled reserves will be higher
- SCR will increase if expense assumptions increase as stresses will be applied to higher liabilities 
- Overall SCR coverage is expected to reduce due to higher liabilities and higher SCR
- But depends whether new outsourcer in found in a reasonable period of time.

**Credit default spreads widening and inflation increasing**
- Assets will reduce due to spreads widening.
- Any inflation linked assets will increase in value.
- No change in liabilities if valued using risk free rate under SII.
- So have a mismatch between assets and liabilities.
- Liabs will increase due to inflation rising due to any inflation linked benefits.
- Impact on SCR is uncertain as asset stresses will reduce;
- but liabilities stresses will increase.

<u><b>Q2(v): Discuss, with reference to the combinations in part (iv), why the combined impact of the two risk events may differ from the sum of the impact of each event in isolation.</b></u>
- The level of the stress in the combined scenario may differ from the individual scenario.
- The stress may not be linear;
- so if the stress reduces by 50% the impact may not reduce by 50%.
- The interaction of stresses in the combined scenario may change the impact for example due to non-separability of stress;
- or diversification between risks.
- For example, credit defaults and inflation may have some diversification.
- For new business vols it may be that the longevity risk differs based on the scheme;
- for example they may have assumed more deferred member schemes are written as vols increase.

<u><b>Q2(vi): ) Set out, with reasons, possible actions the company could take to reduce its exposure to each of the above risks.</b></u>
**Longevity**
- Use longevity reinsurance/hedge;
- to reduce the exposure to the company of longevity differing from expected.
- This will come at a cost in terms of a fee;
- but as longevity is a key risk it may be beneficial.
- Will reduce SCR but with a small offset due to counterparty risk increasing.

**Credit Spreads**
- Buy assets with good credit ratings.
- Apply for a matching adjustment;
- which would allow the firm to value liabs using a higher RDR than the risk free rate;
- which would reduce liabs.
- Also, the MA would reflect the assets held
- so the assets and liabs would move together reducing risk;
- better matching of assets to liabilities.

**Inflation**
- Purchase inflation hedges;
- to reduce exposure to higher inflation than expected.
- Match with indexed bonds
