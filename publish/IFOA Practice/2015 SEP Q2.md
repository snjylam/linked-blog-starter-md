<u><b>Q2(ii): Describe the additional risks to the company in entering into the outsourcing contract.</b></u>
 **Counterparty risk**
- There is a risk that the outsourcer failing to perform its contractual obligations; 
- or does not perform them in a timely manner.
- There is a risk if required, the company is not able to bring back administration in-house quickly leading to business disruption;
- or find another outsourcing company quickly enough and at an acceptable price.

**Operational risk**
- There is a risk that the staff administering the business are not sufficiently skilled; 
- particularly in understanding the products;
- leading to fall of service standards and mistakes;
- leading to complaints.
- There is a risk that the customer service availability may not be as effective than at present.
- The risk is exacerbated if customers have contact with the outsourcing staff only.
- There is a risk that the outsourcer does not treat customers fairly;
- leading to reputational damage;
- reduction in future new business and withdrawals;
- particularly for group pension products where administration services are critical.
- The lower levels of new business and increased withdrawals may lead to risk of not meeting overhead expenses.
- There is a risk of redundancies of current staff, or transfer of current staff to the outsourcer.
- There is a risk of lack of controls in the outsourcer's unit pricing function;
- which could lead to the risk of providing compensation for any unit pricing errors;
- and costs in rectifying client records.
- Any unit pricing error would increase risk of regulatory intervention.
- There is a risk of IT errors or delays from any transfer of data from company's systems to outsourcer's systems; 
- which may increase costs and reduce service standards. 
- There is risk of the systems being incompatible with each other (e.g. Financial with Actuarial Valuation systems)
- There is a risk of delays in system developments which may put future new business or product developments at risk.
- There is risk that management's focus is directed away from generating new business as they may be preoccupied in dealing with the outsourcing arrangements and any problems that may arise.

**Legal risk**
- The outsourcing contract will introduce legal risk.
- The contract will be for a specific period and likely have a schedule of changes to be made.
- The company will be exposed to uncertainties that arise at the end of the contract; with the potential need for in-sourcing or alternative outsourcing.
