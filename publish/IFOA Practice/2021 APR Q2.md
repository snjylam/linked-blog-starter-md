<u><b>Q2(ii): Discuss the components that are likely to be included in an analysis of change of Solvency II BEL.</u></b>
**Opening adjustments**
- This starts with the BEL at the end of the previous period.
- allowing for opening adjustments, i.e. model changes;
- or regulatory changes.
- These adjustments may be shown separately and can be calculated at the end of the analysis period rather than at the start.

**Expected change in BEL over period**
- This is the roll-forward of the BEL allowing for expected investment return, i.e. 'unwind' of discount rate.
- which is based on the risk-free rate
- possibly with a matching or volatility adjustment.
- When rolling forward the BEL it will be reduced to allow for expected mortality;
- increased for expected benefit inflation;
- increased for expected expense inflation;
- reduced due to expected benefit payments;
- and reduced due to expense cashflows.

**Experience variances**
- The experience variance includes impact of mortality experience;
- i.e. the actual deaths differing from expected;
- which results in different number of actual in-force policies at end of period versus expected.
- The experience variance will also include:
	- difference between actual and expected expense inflation;
	- difference between actual and expected benefit inflation.
- The mortality experience relating to dependants might be identified separately.
- There is additional variance due to difference between actual and assumed marital status;
- and other dependant details (e.g. age).

**Assumption changes**
- The assumption changes include changes in the discount rate used for BEL;
- including changes to matching or volatility adjustment used;
- or introduction of a matching or volatility adjustment not previously approved.
- Other changes in best estimate assumptions in BEL include:
	- future mortality / longevity experience 
	- future maintenance expenses 
	- future expense inflation
	- future benefit inflation.
- A change in the view of future longevity might have been driven by general trends;
- or due to specific details relating to the mortality of these scheme members.

**New business contribution** 
- This includes BEL for any new schemes written over the period.

**Unexplained** 
- This is the balancing item, enabling the full change to be categorised.

**Closing position** 
- This is the BEL as reported at the end of the analysis period

<u><b>Q2(iii): Discuss how each item outlined above impacts the analysis of change in the BEL.</u></b>
**Stronger reserving assumption for mortality improvements.**
- This is included in ‘Changes in assumptions’.
- The BEL will increases due to the annuity expected to be paid for longer, so a positive change amount.

**Higher than expected expenses** 
- This is included in ‘Changes in assumptions’;
- if the expense over-run relates to maintenance rather than initial expenses;
- and the higher expense level is expected to continue in future.
- The BEL will increase, so a positive change amount.

**Higher than expected deaths on one scheme** 
- This is included in ‘Experience variances’.
- The BEL will decrease, due to fewer in-force policies, so a negative change amount.
- If the company increases its assumptions as a result of the additional deaths;
- it would be reflected in ‘Changes in assumptions’;
- The BEL will further decrease, so a negative change amount. 

<u><b>Q2(iv): Suggest possible reasons why the estimated BEL determined by pricing differs from actual BEL by reserving.</u></b>
**Cashflows**
- The two models might differ:
	- e.g. having different cashflow projection frequencies;
	- e.g. one model assumes all cashflows occur mid-month, whereas the other at more accurate timings.
	- because the models have been built for different purposes. 
- The benefit cashflow projections used to determine the BEL might differ:
	- e.g. since pricing would be done scheme by scheme, the pricing model might assume that the benefits inflate at the correct date for each particular scheme; 
	- whereas the reserving model might be set up to inflate all schemes at the same time.
- Alternatively, pricing model may not need to be as accurate as reserving model when determining BEL.
- There may be more approximations in the BEL calculation within the pricing model.
- There may have been changes to the reserving cashflow projection methodology since the pricing model was set up.
- There might be an error in one of the models.

**Assumptions** 
- Assumptions might be applied differently;
	- e.g. the pricing model might use a single discount rate, whilst the reserving model might use a yield curve.
	- e.g. one model might use a single assumed inflation rate, the other might use an inflation curve.
- The pricing team may not allow accurately for any matching or volatility adjustment.
- There may be timing differences relating to the assumptions;
- resulting in different assumptions being used in the two models.
- Different assumptions might be made in relation to dependants, e.g. the proportion of married members. 

**Data**  
- Scheme data might have been interpreted differently by the two teams.
- The pricing model might use grouped data for the BEL calculation, rather than individual member data.
- The data used might differ, e.g. slightly different dates.
