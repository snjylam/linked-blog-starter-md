<u><b>Q2(i): Suggest reasons for the change in IFRS profits since 2014.</b></u>
**Lower profits** 
- Individuals no longer have to purchase annuities with their accumulated pension funds;
- resulting in falling new business levels.
- Competitors may have taken new business by offering products with innovative features or rates that are more attractive. 
- Falling new business levels would have contributed to the falling IFRS profits;
- if new business was profitable under IFRS.
- For no changes / increase in sales volumes, higher new business strain could cause profits to fall.
- However, given recent pension legislative changes, this is unlikely. 
- The company would be exposed to volatile profits if it chose to invest in risky assets.
- Any mismatch in assets and liabilities will affect the profit trend. 

**Non-economic experience** 
- Losses may have been made due to poor claim experience;
- e.g. fewer deaths than expected;
- or higher expenses than expected.
- These would affect level of profits and volatility.

**Economic experience**
- The company may have invested in risky assets;
- which suffered an increase in defaults on backing assets;
- or downgrades during this period.
- This would affect profit volatility.
- The company may have changed its accounting methodology to make IFRS reserves more in line with Solvency II, affecting profits in the year of change.
- The company may have changed its asset mix;
- in order to meet the criteria for the Solvency II matching adjustment;
- and this may have indirectly affected the liabilities under IFRS.
- Any change would affect the profit volatility. 
- But this will depend on how matched the IFRS assets and liabilities are.

**Assumptions**
- The company may have changed its best estimate assumptions;
- affecting IFRS reserves and causing profits to be volatile in the year of change.
- The company may have changed its prudential margins in its profit cashflows, affecting volatility in the year of change. 
- If these margins were reduced, this may be a reason for the downward trend in profit.
- The inflation assumption could have been strengthened, increasing volatility in the year of change.

**Development costs**
- There may have been one-off development costs;
- such as Solvency II implementation;
- or work in anticipation of IFRS 17.
- This would have an impact on the volatility of profits.

**Strategic decisions**
- The company may have entered into a longevity swap to hedge the risk from its immediate annuities. 
- This would affect the volatility of profits in the year of change;
- as the reserves would change to reflect the payable fixed swap rates as opposed to the variable rates linked to the mortality book. 
- As actual experience will now equal expected, this will have an impact on the profit trend.

**Other**
- There may be errors in the calculation.

- The business is protection business;
- and so will be treated as non-BLAGAB for tax purposes. 
- Tax will be charged on pre-tax trading profits from the accounts; 
- using the corporation tax rate.
- The pre-tax accounting profit will comprise: Premiums + investment return (including change in asset value) – claims – expenses +/–change in reserves. 
- The corporation tax rate will be the full tax rate applied in territory X. 
- The accounts are likely to be prepared in accordance with IFRS or local GAAP.
- There must be consistency between income and gains included in the revenue account and the increase in the policy liabilities from investment income on premiums;
- meaning that realisable and unrealisable gains are both taxable and dividends are not treated as exempt. 
- However, the local GAAP rules may differ. 

**Policyholder taxation**
1. Premiums will come out of post tax earnings
2. There is unlikely to be material surrender value 
3. so there is unlikely to be taxed on claim.
