<u><b>Q2(i): Describe the main risks to the life insurance company associated with this product.</b></u>
**Market risk**  
- The only charge is the management charge, and this is linked to fund size and so fund performance;
- if returns are less than expected then income;
- and, hence, profits will be less than expected. 

**Persistency risk**
- There is a 5% of fund surrender charge - unlikely to recoup set up costs;
- early in the policy;
- leading to a loss.
- so, the company is at risk of higher than expected surrenders early in the policy term.
- There is also the risk that the customer reduces/stops the premiums;
- which would reduce the management charge income.
- At later durations, the 5% surrender penalty is likely to be a source of profit;
- and so is at risk of lower than expected surrenders;
- at later durations.

**Expense risk**
- The management charge does not match the costs of the company well.
- Higher set up costs are incurred when the management charge is low.
- The outsource per policy fee charge, will be high relative to the management charge early in the policy
- especially for smaller policies;
- but later in the policy, the management charge is likely to be much higher.
- The outsource administration agreement and the investment management agreement are likely to be subject to periodic review;
- and so, the costs incurred by the company after such reviews is unknown.
- The company is likely to retain some costs (corporate, marketing, sales, finance) which may be more fixed in nature
- There is also expense inflation risk.

**Mix of business**
- The cost of the investment management services depends on the funds chosen by customers;
- and yet the income is the same for all funds;
- so, the company is at risk of customers choosing funds with higher costs associated with them than expected.
- The risk of customers choosing shorter term policies than expected;
- whilst some costs (e.g. Commission) may depend on term.
- This contract is likely to be more profitable for longer duration policies.
- The risk of the mix having a lower-than-expected premium size;
- due to the fixed size of some costs.
- Larger policies are likely to be more profitable.
- Low volumes can be a risk for recovering expenses.

**Counterparty risk**
- The company has major operations outsourced and so is at risk of these outsource providers failing. 

**Operational risk**
- Depending on the due diligence in selecting the outsource providers, this may be increased or mitigated by the outsource arrangements.
- Outsource arrangements do result in admin risk.
- Other example: unit pricing risk.
- Mispricing risk, mis selling.

**Legal risk**
- This could arise due to failings in the contract wording with the customer;
- or with the contracts with the outsource providers.

<u><b>Q2(ii): Assess the proposed new charging structure from the perspective of the company.</b></u>
- The revised charging structure better matches the costs incurred;
- and so is likely to require lower capital;
- so, whilst the expected per policy profitability is the same;
- the lower capital requirement may enable more sales;
- and hence more overall profit.
- May need a minimum premium else fund may be low.
- Consider the revised timing of profit emergence.
- In addition, it may be that this charging structure means that the level of profitability is less sensitive to changes in key assumptions;
- and changes in mix of business.
- The company will need to work with its outsource provider to ensure that they are capable of administering this revised structure;
- and if so, whether their fee for the service is different.
- The company may wish to do consumer testing to take into consideration the target market.
- Projections of benefits are likely to be lower on the new charging structure at short durations;
- and higher at longer durations;
- as a result, it may attract a different group of customers;
- which may mean the persistency would be different.
- The product designs of major competitors would need to be considered.
- Is the new style different to the market;
- in which case might it establish a niche;
- or be so out of line as to be unpopular;
- so NB volumes low;
- impacting profits.
- This will be particularly true if the distribution method is through independent financial advisors.
- It may be that the lower levels of surrender values in early years may be considered unfair to customers.
- Regulation may prohibit this charging structure.
- May need to train staff;
- and update systems.


<u><b>Q2(iii): Discuss the suggestion to pay the higher of the value of units at maturity and sum of premium paid.</b></u>
**Popular with customers as it reduced their exposure to market risk.**
- This money back guarantee is likely to be popular with customers;
- especially if this is unique in the market;
- as it reduces the risk to them of falling markets just before maturity;
- increases sales/profits.

**Introduce a charge for this guarantee** 
- However, the company will almost certainly want to make a charge for this guarantee;
- which may reduce the appeal to customers;
- as the charge will reduce the likely payout overall;
- and may lead to complaints for low returns.
- The company will need to carefully price any charge;
- as the charge will reduce the value of units at maturity;
- thus increasing the chance that the guarantee will apply.
- The company may want to limit the guarantee;
- so that it does not apply to any increments in premiums in the last few years;
- as these increments will generate little charge;
- and the value of units purchased by these increments are unlikely to be able to recover from falls in unit prices.
- The company may wish to restrict the unit funds that the customer could select to the fund choices with lower investment risk;
- This may be appropriate as the customers who select the higher risk fund choices are likely to have a higher risk appetite;
- and so are less concerned about having a guarantee;
- whereas this may attract customers with a lower risk appetite.
- The assumptions may need to be revised;
- specifically, the persistency assumptions;
- as customers are less likely to surrender their policy towards the end of the policy;
- if it looks like the guarantee will bite.
- The company may wish, if it does not already, to make the persistency assumptions dynamic;
- so whilst the addition of the guarantee will increase market risk;
- it may reduce persistency risk.

**TCF and operational considerations**
- The company will want to amend its documentation provided to customers
- to clearly explain the guarantee
- at the point of sale
- and on any surrender quote
- so that the customer is aware to the guarantee that they are giving up.
- Are there available hedges/derivatives to match the embedded guarantee?
- It will increase the capital requirement
- If only on lower risk funds, then the lower future fund values may reduce profits.
- Consider the impact on administration systems.
- Stochastic modelling of guarantees will be needed which will lead to extra costs.
- There is increased complexity in payout and so increased operational risk.
