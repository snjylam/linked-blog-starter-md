<u><b>Q1(i): Outline the considerations for the company when selecting a distribution channel for the new term assurance product.</b></u>
**New distribution channels**
- Term assurance is a simple product;
- and pricing is competitive;
- so the costs associated with the distribution should be low.
- The company should consider the target market.
- For the mass market, direct marketing via the internet and telesales may be appropriate.
- It can have simple underwriting e.g. online application form.
- It can be included on price comparison websites.
- It can be marketed to existing policyholders via mass mailing or telesales.
- For the high net worth or as key man insurance, financial advisers may be appropriate.

**Existing distribution channels**
- The company sells personal pension products via advisers;
- and would have used advisers or other distribution channel for its with-profits business;
- e.g. tied agents, multi-tied agents, bancassurance.
- The company could consider using these existing relationships;
- but commission rates would have to be low;
- which may not be attractive to advisers and agents.
- These existing channels may not be appropriate for its desired target market.

**Other considerations**
- The company should consider the costs associated with setting up the distribution channel.
- The company should consider any regulatory constraints regarding distribution and ensure robust sales.
- The company should consider what channels competitors are using.
- The demographic experience is likely to differ by distribution channel.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Suggest how the company might address the lack of data when pricing the new term assurance product.</b></u>
**Internal data**
- It could consider the data from the unit-linked product;
- but adjustments need to be made;
- to reflect the different target market;
- and the distribution channel for the term assurance product.
- The main issue is mortality data;
- and the data from the unit-linked pension may be relevant if the age profile is similar to that of the term assurance product. 
- There may be still relevant mortality data from the with-profits product.
- The expense data from its products may be relevant, with adjustment.

**External data**
- It could consider the data from external sources;
- e.g. data from reinsurers;
- and industry data;
- but adjustments need to be made to reflect the lives of the target market compared to the lives in the external data.
- It could consider a standard mortality table
- based on the lives it insures.
- It could use consultants to assist with pricing.
- It could compare its premium rates with competitors.

**Other actions**
- It could include a higher risk margin in pricing to reflect data uncertainty;
- but the extent to which it can be done is limited to needing to keep pricing competitive.
- It could make premiums reviewable.
- It could place an upper limit on the term assurance policies it writes.

<u><b>Q1(iii): Discuss the actions that the company might take following this announcement to require more capital to be held.</b></u>
**Initial analysis**
- The company should first analyse the expected impact of the regulatory change on the required capital for its with-profits portfolio.
- Subsequent actions will depend on the expected size of the impact on required capital in five years’ time.
- The company might not take any further actions:
	- if the impact is likely to be relatively small or short-term 
	- if the company has a high level of available capital that would easily absorb the impact. 
- The impact depends on the expected size of the with-profits business in five years’ time.
- If the additional required capital is expected to be material, a range of actions may be considered. 
- The company should bear in mind that the additional capital would be expected to be released over the remaining duration of the with-profits business.
- The company should consider whether the new regulations will impact any of its other product lines.
- The analysis should account for any transitional arrangements that are permitted when the new regulations are brought in.

**Bonuses and payouts**
- The higher capital requirements are likely to arise in relation to the guarantees within with-profits business.
- Therefore the company could reduce the level of reversionary (or other guaranteed) bonuses,
- and shift the balance more towards terminal bonus.
- The company could reduce the degree of smoothing it applies to bonuses and payouts, to reduce the capital required to support smoothing.
- The extent of the guarantees inherent in the with-profits business relates to:
	- the original sum assured (or equivalent, if accumulating with-profits), 
	- reversionary (or equivalent) bonuses declared to date,
	- expected future reversionary (or equivalent) bonuses,
	- the duration or outstanding term of the policies.
- Increasing the proportion of terminal bonus reduces the build-up of guarantees and so should reduce required capital.
- The company may make changes to bonus rates immediately, rather than waiting until the new regulations come into effect.
- It may not be possible to make material changes to guarantees for existing business.
- The company might decide to target asset shares more closely when setting MVRs.
- However, applying less smoothing (in general) is unlikely to have a significant impact on mitigating this regulatory change,
- as it mainly impacts the terminal bonus, which is not guaranteed.

**Investment strategy** 
- The company could change the mix of assets backing the with-profits portfolio.
- It could move towards a more matched, lower risk investment strategy in order to reduce required capital.
- Derivatives could be used to hedge the guarantees.
- Changing the investment mix could allow a higher discount rate to be used for valuing liabilities, thus increasing available capital.

**Raise capital**
- The company may need to raise new capital to cover the additional capital requirements. 
- If so, it needs to identify an appropriate source and start to put in place the actions needed.
- For example, it may decide to raise capital through a subordinated debt issue.
- If the company is a mutual, it might consider demutualisation.

**Other possible actions** 
- The company could sell the portfolio of with-profits business;
- or look to convert it to without-profits business.
- Charges could be increased;
- with the justification being that the cost of capital will be increasing in future. 
- This would improve the future capital position of the company;
- but could go against policyholders’ expectations;
- depending on the extent to which charges have been varied in the past. 
- Customer documentation may also restrict the ability to change charges. 
- There may be actions that could be taken to improve capital efficiency;
- such as reducing expenses or making improvements to the regulatory capital model. 
- The company could lobby the regulator against the proposed changes.

**General considerations** 
- The actions need to be consistent with the PPFM or equivalent customer-facing literature;
- and must treat policyholders fairly.
- Approval may be needed from the With-Profits Committee.
- Where relevant the With-Profits Actuary (or equivalent) should be involved.
- The impact of any proposed action should be modelled;
- which may require changes to models.
- The speed at which possible actions can be implemented should also be taken into consideration. 
- Some actions will need to be communicated clearly to the with-profits policyholders.
