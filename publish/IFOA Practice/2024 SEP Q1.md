<u><b>Q1(i): Explain why the reserves may initially be negative.</u></b>
- Reserves will be negative if PV future premiums exceeds PV future benefits
- plus PV future expenses.
- In early years, premiums will be expected to be paid for a long period
- and PV premiums likely to exceed PV future outgo
- e.g. due to profit margins / prudent assumptions.
- and due to expense loadings to recoup initial expenses.
- Negative reserves could be offset partially by SVs in early years.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Suggest why ABC would want to release the value of the negative asset.</u></b>
- A negative reserve is an asset.
- Restricting the negative asset to zero is reducing the asset;
- and reducing the solvency position of the fund.
- The company may want to improve the solvency position if the solvency ratio is below or close to the target level.
- It may be looking to write new business but require more capital to do so.
- Shareholders prefer strong solvency position.
- May attract more new policyholders.
- Wants to avoid takeover.
- To avoid regulator intervention.
- Improve share price.
- Improve working capital.
- Improve credit rating so receive better terms.
- May have liquidity issues it needs cash for.
- May be looking to purchase another firm.
- If new business vols are lower than expected then this may have a second order impact on expenses
- as expenses will be spread over fewer policies.
- Writing more new Whole of Life products will create an even larger negative reserve for a period
- so they are wanting to offset that partially.
- Involves a degree of risk transfer

<u><b>Q1(iv): Discuss the risks of entering this arrangement from the perspective of ABC and the reinsurer.</u></b>
**Reinsurer**
- There is a risk that experience turns out to be worse than expected.
- Mortality rates could be higher than expected so death benefits are paid out earlier.
- Lapses may differ from expected.
- Early lapses lead to less future premiums being received;
- so profits are lower than expected.
- Lapses in later years creates a profit as reserves are positive and will be released.
- Lapses could be driven by poor customer service by the insurer.
- Surrenders in the early years are likely to create a loss as the return of premiums is likely to be higher than the asset share;
- Higher surrenders in the early years create a loss.
- Expenses may be higher than expected.
- The insurer may default on payments of future profits to the reinsurer.
- There is an opportunity cost if interest rates are higher than assumed;
- so the value of the future profits are less than the amount paid upfront to the insurer.
- If interest rates turn out to be high, then the reinsurer may have made a larger profit by investing the money rather than lending it to the insurer.
- This may be due to incorrect modelling.
- There is legal risk associated with the contract.

**Insurer**
- Risk is limited as lump sum is paid out upfront.
- But there is a risk that experience is better than expected;
- and so the insurer loses out on future profits being higher than it expected.
- The insurer is exposed to operational risks, such as modelling errors leading to incorrect profits being passed to the reinsurer.

<u><b>Q1(v): Discuss the reasons to allocate the indirect costs on the expected number of policies in 5 years time.</u></b>
**Reasons for**
- The proposal makes sense if volumes are expected to increase significantly and be stable in 5 yrs 
- so setting long term assumption based on stable policy counts 
- as fixed costs will be spread over more policies 
- resulting in a lower expense assumption per product
- but need to consider the stability of the policy counts on the other products too.

**Reasons against**
- Expense reserves will be too low initially 
- as modelled expenses will be less than expected expenses 
- as policy counts will be below the levels expected in 5 years
- so they will be under reserving.
- or will need to hold an extra reserve for the shortfall between now and 5 years 
- There is risk that NB vols are below targets 
- as it will be hard to predict vols in 6 yrs time
- so again, under reserve.
- May results in a variance in P&L
- as actual expenses will be higher than expected expenses.
- May not be in line with the regulations.
