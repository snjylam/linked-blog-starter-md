<u><b>Q1(i): Suggest possible tax incentives the government could use to encourage people to save for retirement.</b></u>
- It could offer tax relief on contributions by the employee.
- It could offer tax relief on premiums paid by the employer.
- It could not charge investment income.
- It could not charge capital gains on the assets.
- It could allow the benefits to be taken in a tax advantageous way
- e.g. part of the pension to be taken as a tax-free cash sum.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Suggest possible restrictions on the tax incentives that the government might consider.</b></u>
- There may be limits on contributions paid into the scheme.
- There may be limits on the total amount invested and accumulated within the scheme
- so when benefits are taken, the excess over the limit is taxed
- at a higher rate.
- It may place restrictions on the product design
- e.g. it may insist products require approval
- e.g. it may restrict the type of assets that can be held by the scheme
- e.g. it may may require policyholders to purchase an annuity on retirement
- e.g. it may limit the amount of tax-free cash sum that can be taken on retirement.
- It may allow tax relief on contributions below a certain age, e.g. the retirement age.

<u><b>Q1(iii): What are the risks to the policyholder associated with a unit-linked individual pension product?</b></u>
**Meeting customer needs**
- The pension funds at retirement may not be enough to meet the policyholder's needs;
- for example if the policyholder invested too little;
- or charges e.g. AMC, may have been too high especially when not guaranteed;
- or worsening annuity rates at retirement;
- or due to inflation eroding real value. 
- The tax regime may have changed and became more restrictive;
- so the policyholder has to invest more into the fund;
- to make up for the tax relief lost.
- The policyholder bears the risk of investments not perform as well as expected;
- for example, the company's funds may perform poorly relative to the market;
- e.g. a lack of diversification;
- leading to lower fund than expected at retirement.
- There may have been a market crash just before retirement;
- leaving too little time to benefit from the recovery.
- The product may have been mis-sold;
- or the policyholder does not understand the product. 
- There may be excessive penalties or charges if the policyholder changes their mind;
- or wishes to transfer to another policyholder.
- The transfer process may be too slow or complicated.
- The policyholder's circumstances may have changed suddenly such that the policyholder needs money now.
- The policyholder's circumstances may have changed and have to reduce the premium. 
- Overall the policy could result in poor value for money.

**Actions of the company**
- The policyholder may have received poor marketing material or advice;
- and so did not know what to expect.
- The customer service may be poor; 
- so the customer would not know how the policy is performing.
- The company may become insolvent;
- leading to uncertainty of the security of benefits.
- The company may not have sold sufficient volumes;
- which may lead to the company increasing charges. 
- There may be regulatory changes that impact benefits.

<u><b>Q1(iv): Set out actions the company should take following unit pricing error.</u></b>
- The company should inform the regulator;
- and set out its plan to rectify the error.
- The company should inform policyholders; 
- and set up a customer services team to handle calls and complaints.
- The company should identify which in-force policies have been impacted
- as well as historic policies;
- and calculate the correct unit prices;
- and ensure claim values in the future are correct.
- Premiums received will have been credited with too many units (because unit price too low);
- and the company should decide whether to rectify this as well;
- e.g. by considering the materiality of the error.  
- The company should investigate how the error occurred.
- and incorporate appropriate controls to minimise the risk of it happening again.
- If material and the company is listed, the company should consider whether to inform the stock exchange;
- and any news media calls. 
- If there are changes to policyholder benefits may lead to additional tax liability;
- the company may have to cover this difference;
- in consultation with the tax authorities.

<u><b>Q1(v): Discuss how the company's Solvency II balance sheet will change now the error has been rectified.</u></b>
- The asset values will be lower:
	- due to rectification payments made;
	- compensation for the under-credited units;
	- any tax payments on behalf of policyholders;
	- any project costs of calculating the corrections;
	- and any cost in relation to policyholder calls/complaints.
- The best estimate liabilities will increase overall: 
	- the unit reserve will be higher due to the higher corrected unit prices, increasing BEL;
	- the value of future fund related charges, will increase based on the higher unit fund values, reducing BEL;
	- however, the increase in the unit reserve is likely to outweigh the benefit from higher future charges;
	- so overall BEL will rise.
- The risk margin may increase if the unit fund is used as a driver in the RM calculation.
- The own funds will likely be reduced as fall in asset value and increase in technical provisions will result in lower NAV in the Solvency II balance sheet.
