<u><b>Q2(ii): Discuss why the company’s economic capital requirements are lower than its regulatory capital requirements.</b></u>
- Economic capital - is an internal determination of the capital required.
- Regulatory capital - solvency requirements prescribed by the regulators.
- They may target different measure of security;
- or different level.
- For UL business the Solvency II future profits could be constrained by contract boundaries.
- Economic capital may take account of projected charges less expenses on premiums past the contract boundary.
- Solvency II requires the use of a risk-free rate;
- economic capital could make use of a liquidity premium in its calculations;
- which means that projected unit linked charges may be higher;
- or the yield curve may be different.
- Income protection contracts are complex;
- the allowance for morbidity may differ between Solvency II and economic capital;
- or the allowance for recovery rates may be higher under economic capital;
- because the company may have plans to improve underwriting at point of inception;
- and during benefit payouts;
- which may not be appropriate to take into account under solvency capital.
- More generally, the regulator may have asked the company to hold a capital add-on.
- Could take the form of higher stresses;
- or different correlations;
- or different allowance for operational risk.
- Economic capital may assume a lower cost of capital on the SCR;
- so the risk margin could be lower.
- Economic capital may consider new business strategies.
- Regulatory capital may restrict the use of certain assets.

<u><b>Q2(iv): Discuss how the company could mitigate the potential climate risks it is exposed to.</b></u>
**Income Protection Product**
- It is unlikely that exclusions could be applied for morbidity related to climate change.
- Reprice IP products to make premiums reviewable.
- For new business ensure underwriting considers the initial stages of climate related health issues;
- such as illness related to pollution;
- or some health problems may be exacerbated by climate change;
- in which case any loadings need to be reconsidered.
- Update underwriting ratings for changes to risks due to climate change;
- e.g. different occupations may become more risky due to climate change;
- e.g. firemen;
- or risks associated with locations may change.

**UL Product**
- Reduce death guarantees on new UL products if these are onerous;
- most people use UL for savings so this may not impact new business.
- Diversify assets;
- e.g. consider a broader range of asset sectors;
- reduce / avoid assets particularly exposed to climate - e.g. reduce exposure to assets linked to fossil fuels;
- invest in 'greener' assets to follow consumer trends.
- Use the results of scenario testing to identify key risks and consider changes to products or new products.
- Keep abreast of thinking on the impact of climate change;
- and any changes in regulations;
- work directly with government / regulator to understand future changes in climate regulation that may impact insurer to give insurer sufficient time to react;
- reinsurance which covers climate risk;
- support from consultants/climate modelling experts.
- Operational - have appropriate business interruption plans if adverse physical climate event interrupts operations.
- However this may not be compliant with regulations.

<u><b>Q2(v): Justify why scenario testing may be more appropriate than stochastic modelling to assess the potential impact of climate change.</b></u>
- The scale and timing of the impact of climate change are uncertain.
- There is no historic data on which to calibrate a stochastic model;
- even if there was past data there is no guarantee it will be appropriate for the future.
- The distribution of risks due to climate change are not clearly understood;
- the interdependencies between the risks is also not well understood;
- therefore it is impractical to model this stochastically.
- It is particularly difficult to assess the impact of climate change in the tail of liabilities which is most relevant for assessing solvency.
- The risks cannot be managed through capital markets/hedged;
- meaning that a longer time period is more appropriate.
- Communicating the potential risks associated with climate change are easier to explain through scenarios;
- to the Board;
- and the regulator.
- Scenario testing helps with risk identification;
- scenario testing also allows company to determine broadly which climate risks are likely to have biggest impact on company.
- Scenario modelling feasible to complete during tight reporting timelines, as quicker run time.
