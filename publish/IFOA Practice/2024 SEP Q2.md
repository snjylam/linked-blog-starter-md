<u><b>Q2(i): Explain how these products would be accounted for following introduction of IFRS17.</b></u>
**Variable Fee Approach**
- The Variable Fee Approach (VFA) would be used;
- as the policyholder participates in a share; 
- of a clearly defined pool of assets.
- The company will hold 
	- best estimate liabilities (BEL);
	- risk adjustment (RA);
	- contractual service margin (CSM).
- The discount rate used under this approach is determined with reference to that pool of assets.
- The CSM is unlocked and adjusted for any market effects, and are smoothed over time to fall into operating profit as the CSM is released. 

<u><b>Q2(iii): Describe for each of these two products how best estimate liabilities would be determined under Solvency II.</b></u>
- The contract boundary;
- would be the maturity date for the conventional with-profits endowment;
- but the future premiums may not be able to be taken into account for the unit-linked endowment
- depending on the details of the terms and conditions.

**Conventional with-profits endowment assurance**
- The BEL of the conventional with-profits endowment would be split into two parts:
	- guaranteed benefits
	- future discretionary benefits.
- The BEL would need to include an allowance for the market-consistent cost of the guarantees;
- which would be a prospective calculation;
- of the value of the expected cost of any death or maturity benefits in excess of the asset share.
- In practice the BEL is likely to start from the current asset share; 
- allowing for any expected distribution from the estate;
- as the expectation is that the asset share will be paid out.
- The cost of death and maturity guarantees will use best estimate assumptions;
- and a risk-free discount curve;
- allowing for management actions;
- and expected policyholder behaviour;
- and will also allow for smoothing.
- Shareholder transfers in respect of future bonus declarations are not included in the BEL.
- The allowance for the distribution of the estate should be in line with local regulations.
- Projections will be on a policy-by-policy basis.
- However, approximations are permitted and grouped model points can be used provided certain conditions are met

**Unit-linked endowment assurance**
- The BEL of the unit-linked endowment would also be split into two parts:
	- the unit liability
	- the non-unit liability.
- The unit liability would be the market value of the units at the valuation date. 
- The non-unit liability would be a prospective valuation of future expenses less charges;
- including mortality charges and costs if there is a mortality benefit different to the unit-value.
- It is unlikely that there would be any maturity guarantees that would need to be valued.
- Guarantees are valued stochastically.

<u><b>Q2(iv): Suggest possible reasons for the fall in BEL for the unit-linked endownment.</b></u>
**Volume of business**
- There may be fewer policies in force.
- Exits may have significantly exceeded new policies;
- which is most likely due to higher lapses;
- which may be due to poor publicity about the company;
- perhaps due to regulatory sanctions.
- Company A may have sold a block of this business to another company.
- The high exits are less likely to be due to mortality;
- as endowments tend to be taken out by younger people;
- although there may have been a pandemic.

**Asset values**
- There may have been a fall in asset values backing the unit-linked liabilities.
- This will directly lead to a fall in the unit-linked liabilities;
- as these are valued at market value.
- However, the fall in unit-linked values will also lead to a fall in expected future charges based on the unit value;
- such as the annual management charge;
- which will increase the value of the non-unit reserve.
- The overall impact of a fall in asset values will depend on the relative sizes of the unit and non-unit reserves;
- but the first order fall in the unit reserve is likely to be the more significant.
- The opposite could be true for new business.

**Discount rate**
- The discount rate may have changed.
- The direction of the change in the discount rate resulting in a fall in BEL will depend on whether the non-unit reserves are positive or negative.
- If the non-unit reserves are negative (charges exceeds costs), then a fall in the discount rate will lead to the non-unit reserves becoming more negative;
- reducing the BEL.
- Alternatively, if the non-unit reserves are positive (costs exceed charges), then a rise in the discount rate will reduce the non-unit reserves;
- and, more widely, lead to a fall in asset values;
- possibly reducing the unit reserves as well.
- If the unit fund is invested in bonds, then higher interest rates will lead to a lower value of these bonds.

**Assumptions**
- The assumptions may have changed.
- A lower mortality assumption;
- would lead to lower expected mortality claims;
- and policies expected to be in force longer as policyholders live longer.
- A lower expense assumption, or lower inflation assumption, would lead to lower non-unit reserves;
- this could be due to improved efficiency;
- or outsourcing some areas.
- Past experience may have led to a review of the best estimate assumptions since last year;
- with favourable changes to assumptions;
- resulting in a reduction to the non-unit liabilities.
- A lower lapse assumption;
- would again lead to policies being in force longer;
- and so generating more charge income;
- although this would be at odds with the points made above on fewer policies being in force. 

**Other**
- The fall in reserves could be due to changes in reserving regulations/reserving methodology/tax rules.
- Reviewable charges may have been increased.
- There may have been an error in the BEL calculation.

<u><b>Q2(v): Discuss the issues that Company A would need to consider before proceeding with the sale.</b></u>
**Legal process**
- The arrangement for transfer of business may be set out in law;
- with the regulator setting out additional requirements beyond those set in law;
- which aims to protect the customers of both companies.
- The company will need to ensure fair treatment of the transferring policyholders; 
- and fair treatment of the remaining policyholders. 
- The company will need to consider if there are transferring policies covered by multiple jurisdictions;
- as multiple regulators would be involved;
- and there may be restrictions if the buying company is in a different jurisdiction;
- thus limiting the number of potential buyers. 
- The regulator may require the company to appoint an independent expert;
- to consider the fair treatment of all policyholders. 
- A court process may be required.

**Treat customers fairly / policyholder's reasonable expectations**
- The company would need to protect policyholders from the buyer changing the way discretion is applied; for example, to maintain a similar:
	- investment strategy;
	- bonus policy;
	- smoothing policy;
	- surrender terms. 
- If this is only with with-profits, then TCF will be more straightforward;
- but if there is other with-profits business within the with-profits fund, it will be difficult; 
- as it will be difficult to demonstrate fair treatment if the with-profits fund is 'split' by the sale;
- as the sold and remaining groups will have an interest in the estate;
- and so their respective benefit expectations may alter as a result of the sale.
- The buyer may want to move to a less risky investment strategy; 
- to reduce the risk of guarantees biting;
- at the expense of the higher returns that riskier assets may provide for policyholders.
- The buyer may want to change the balance between regular and final bonus to maximise shareholder transfers (if proprietary).
- The buyer may want to be more cautious on estate distributions.
- The fair allocation of expenses to be charged to the with-profits fund will need to be considered.
- All of the above would impact potential payouts to transferred policyholders.
- PRE will be influenced by the PPFM;
- and any past communications to policyholders. 

**Management of the closed fund**
- The company needs to consider the run off plan.
- The company needs to consider whether to distribute the estate prior to the transfer.
- The company needs to consider if the buyer have the necessary experience of managing with-profits (e.g. customer services and other teams).
- The company needs to consider the financial strength of the buyer;
- and if it is weaker than Company A, then there is a potential reduction in the benefit security for the transferred policyholders.
- The company needs to consider the its own financial strength for the remaining policyholders.

**Business case**
- The company would have to consider the business case;
- and whether it aligns with the company's ongoing strategy
- and whether the benefits of the sale outweigh the costs.
- The company would have to consider the impact on its balance sheet and solvency position.
- The company would have to consider the impact on any diversification benefits.
- The company would have to consider the loss of economies of scale and the impact on expense assumptions.

**Practicalities**
- The company needs to consider the timescales involved.
- The company needs to consider other potential buyers.
- The company needs to consider any existing outsource agreements would be transferred to buyer;
- or if the outsourcer maintain services on the remaining policies. 
- If so, transfer will be simpler;
- as no data migration;
- so less operational risk involved.
- The extent that the outsourcer would still be involved would be reflected in the price of the sale of the business.
- The company needs to consider alternative developments now the business has been sold.
- The company needs to consider the views of the Chief Actuary/With-Profits Actuary/With-Profits Committee.
