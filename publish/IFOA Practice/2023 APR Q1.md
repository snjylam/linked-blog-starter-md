<u><b>Q1(i): Discuss possible reasons for significant positive non-economic variance.</u></b>
**Mortality**
- There may have been more deaths than expected;
- e.g. due to a pandemic, emergence of new diseases.
- For the annuity business, higher deaths would lead positive variance;
- as reserves held for annuitants who have died are released.
- For the pensions business, the impact of higher deaths on variance depends on the death benefit;
- which is usually a return of fund;
- so fewer deaths than expected would lead to positive variance due to retaining value on those policies.
- For the term assurance business, higher deaths would lead negative variance;
- although the impact would be small due to the relative size of the business.
- Alternatively, there may have been fewer deaths only for the term assurance leading to positive variance.
- Overall, the impact from annuities is likely the most significant, therefore it is likely there has been more deaths than expected.

**Persistency**
- There may have been fewer lapses than expected;
- e.g. due to a better retention team/policy or better economic conditions.
- For the annuity business there is no impact from lapses.
- For the pensions business, lower lapses would lead to positive variance;
- as there are more in-force policies at end of year.
- The non-unit reserves are likely determined as the excess of charges less expenses;
- which is likely negative for a profitable product;
- so lower lapses would lead to lower year-end reserves;
- and but also would lead to increased required capital due to more policies in-force.
- Overall increase in required capital is not likely sufficient to offset the reduction in reserves;
- leading to a positive variance.
- For the term assurance business, assuming it is written to be profitable, it would have negative BEL;
- so lower lapses would lead to positive variance.
- Alternatively if the term assurance was not profitable and had positive BEL,
- lower lapses would lead to negative variance.
- Overall, the impact from pensions is likely the most significant, therefore it is likely there has been lower lapses than expected.

**Expenses**
- There may have been lower expenses than expected;
- e.g. due to efficiency programmes delivery lower per policy costs;
- or due to outsourcing arrangements;
- and these have not yet been recognised in the assumptions.

**Overall**
- The variance could have arisen from a release of any margins for uncertainty in assumptions.
- Reinsurance outcomes might have been more favourable than expected. 
- There may have been movements in any ‘risk margin’ component of the liabilities 
- or the required capital, as a result of experience outcomes.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Describe how the three types of business would be reported under IFRS 17.</b></u>
**General Measurement Model**
- The General Measurement Model (GMM);
- would be used for the immediate annuities;
- and for the term assurance product.
- The discount rates under GMM are derived from observable market data.
- There may be a difference between the treatment of the annuities and term assurance;
- as the term assurance may be sold as a loss leader and so would be 'loss-making at inception';
- and would therefore have a 'loss component' rather than a CSM.

**Variable Fee Approach**
- The Variable Fee Approach (VFA) would be used for the pension product;
- since it is unit-linked and so cashflows vary in line with the value of a clearly defined pool of assets.
- The discount rate used under this approach is determined with reference to that pool of assets;
- with the 'pool of assets' being the unit fund(s). 
- Both approaches include a risk adjustment (RA) in the total liabilities;
- and may include a contractual service margin (CSM).
