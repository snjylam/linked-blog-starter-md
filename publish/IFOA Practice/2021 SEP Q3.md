<u><b>Q3(i): Discuss how the government could use taxation measures to promote the domestic life protection market.</u></b>
**Policyholder taxation**
- Give tax relief on protection business premiums;
- in order to make the products more attractive, thus increasing demand for them
- Provide tax breaks to employers purchasing group life cover.
- Reduce the tax payable on benefits received;
- although this might have limited impact since protection benefits are already unlikely to be taxed.
- Tax relief on premiums effectively makes the product cheaper.
- Death benefits may fall within an inheritance tax calculation;
- so the government could change this;
- however, inheritance tax only tends to be incurred by the more affluent;
- and this may not be the population segment that the government wishes to target.
- The government could implement any combination of these measures.

**Life insurance company taxation**
- Reduce the amount of tax payable by life insurance companies that sell protection business;
- such as by allowing more ‘E’ to be deducted, e.g. by not spreading acquisition expenses.
- Simplify the tax treatment of protection business.
- Move protection business to be taxable on a profits rather than BLAGAB-style basis;
- to encourage more competition in the market;
- by ‘levelling the playing field’ and thus encouraging more new entrants specialising in protection business into the market.

<u><b>Q3(ii): Discuss how the regulator of the territory may use regulation to achieve the government’s aim.</u></b>
- Could make it easier for life insurance companies to gain authorisation to sell protection business.
- Simplify distribution regulations to encourage selling through the internet.
- Simplify or relax supervisory reporting requirements and timescales, to make it more attractive for new entrants to the protection market.
- Relax valuation requirements for protection business.
- Reduce the amount of regulatory capital requirements that have to be held for protection business.
- Relax regulatory requirements for reinsurers, to encourage more into the market to support protection business providers.
- Remove or reduce restrictions on product design;
- in order to encourage more innovation in the market.
- Relax any restrictions on underwriting processes.
- Impose a mandatory minimum level of protection cover.
- for example, require employers to provide cover for employees.
- Provide standardised product wordings to make them easier for customers to understand.
- Impose stricter regulatory requirements on foreign insurers, in order to make it easier for domestic insurers to access the market.  
- The actions taken need to balance protecting policyholders;
- and ensuring that the public has faith in the stability of the insurance industry;
- with making it easier for life insurance companies to sell protection business.
- The regulator could:
    - amend authorisation requirements:
        - for example, simply authorise a company to write protection business
        - alternatively, require each new protection product to be authorised
        - but this would likely not help to achieve the objective
        - since it would slow down innovation.
    - amend business standards requirements:
        - for example, simplify conduct rules
        - particularly since term assurance is a relatively simple product
        - but unlikely to relax senior management, systems and controls standards
        - or rules on making complaints
    - amend valuation requirements, such as relaxing:
        - any limitations on assets that can be held to back this business
        - how the assets are valued
        - rules on the treatment of reinsurance
    - amend requirements relating to fit and proper persons, including:
        - that managers are suitably experienced in protection business
        - that there are appropriate supportive statutory actuarial roles in place
    - amend regulations for groups in order to encourage foreign insurance companies to establish a branch or joint venture in this territory, to help to boost the market
    - remove any prescribed premium rates or caps, in order to promote competition
    - prevent the use of certain rating factors or the results of genetic tests, to widen the potential target market and make it more socially inclusive.

<u><b>Q3(iii): Discuss, from a tax perspective, why the company may only sell a limited volume of this savings product.</u></b>
- The company is likely to be XSE. This is because it mainly writes term assurance business;
- which has low ‘I’ due to low levels of reserve and typically low yielding backing assets.
- The savings product will be XSI.
- The excess ‘E’ on the protection business can be offset against the excess ‘I’ on the savings business.
- This either enables a gross roll-up of investment return within the pricing of the savings product;
- which makes the product more attractive to customers;
- or enables a greater profit margin for the insurance company.
- Alternatively, the company might pass the tax benefit achieved on to the term assurance policyholders, by pricing using net (post-tax) rather than gross (untaxed) expenses.
- The company needs to limit sales in order to preserve its XSE position;
- particularly if the tax benefit of this position has been passed to the savings customers.

<u><b>Q3(iv): Discuss the actions that the company may consider in preparation for this change in taxation basis.</u></b>
**Pricing and products** 
- The company needs to consider whether its pricing / premium rates remain appropriate. 
- Its ability to continue to write the savings product on a gross ‘I’ basis will be significantly reduced.
- Therefore the company needs to consider its strategy for single premium savings business. 
- For example, it might even decide to stop selling that product.
- The company is currently likely to be in an XSE position;
- but because future term assurance business will no longer be written in the BLAGAB-style fund;
- it will no longer contribute the previously material amounts of ‘E’ to the ‘I-E’ calculation. 
- The company might decide to restrict the volumes of savings product sold even further;
- or start to sell it on a net ‘I’ basis;
- although that would make its pricing less competitive.
- The company might consider: 
	- product diversification 
	- purchasing a block of business
	- taking over another life insurance company
	- selling out to another company.

**Market changes** 
- The company might wait to see what competitors are doing before taking any action.
- It needs to consider whether there might be any new entrants to the market as a result of the change 
- and, if so, whether it needs to launch its own innovative new products to maintain market share.
- The company should consider the strategic changes that might take place in the industry. 
- There might be an impact on the company’s distribution source.
- There might be product innovation that makes the company’s own products look unattractive.
- It might decide to make changes to its products before the 2026 implementation date;
- in order to get ahead of the competition. 

**Implementation**
- The company needs to update its systems to reflect the change in tax fund for the term assurance business.
- It may need to hire a consultant / tax specialist to help it understand the implications of the changes.
- It should carry out modelling to produce projections of the company’s position;
- particularly of the implications for the company’s tax bill;
- and of profits before and after tax.
- The company needs to update its processes (e.g. tax calculation and reporting) to reflect the change.
- Modelled projections should consider different new business scenarios.
