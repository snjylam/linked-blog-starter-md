<u><b>Q3(i): Explain how the company will ensure it is treating customers fairly.</b></u>
**Customer needs / financial sophistication**
- The company needs to understand who their target market is.
- Young professionals may not have excessive disposable income. 
- The company needs to understand the needs of the target market.
- Young professionals may be aiming to grow their funds reliably; 
- but may have a high risk appetite as they do not have an immediate need for the money.
- Their goal may to buy their first home. 
- The company needs to ensure the product is tailored to those needs. 
- The company needs to understand the financial sophistication of the target market. 
- Young professionals may be financially aware;
- but may be inexperienced with investments. 

**Communications**
- The company needs to ensure product literature and communications are clear.
- For example, it should ensure the guaranteed return of premium is clearly understood.
- The company should consider what advice customers should receive and how they should receive it. 
- It should ensure advisors are well trained;
- and that the sales approach is appropriate. 
- It should include a post-sales cooling off period. 
- The company needs to ensure the charges are transparent and fair. 
- The company should have good customer service;
- and give special consideration to vulnerable customers.

**Risk for policyholders**
- Customers will receive less than their premium on surrender and the company must make sure that this risk is clearly understood by the policyholder.
- The company should ensure that there are no barriers to exit on death or surrender.
- Customers will receive the average index return on maturity, which could be significantly lower than receiving the full index return;
- so the company needs to make sure this is clearly communicated.
- On death late in the term, the death benefit (return of premium) could be significantly lower than the upcoming maturity payment;
- as the company keeps the profit from the derivative payout;
- so the company needs to make sure that customers understand the balance between the different benefits.
- The company will need to ensure the long-term viability of the company and product, so that there is stability throughout the policy lifetime. 
- This will include profitability, competitiveness, level of risk.

<u><b>Q3(ii): Suggest the key considerations in pricing the product.</b></u>
**Costs** 
- The company needs to consider the costs involved in offering the product.
- These include expenses and commission 
- and in particular the costs of developing the new product.
- The company needs to decide whether to price on a marginal cost basis or not.
- It will need to set mortality assumptions
- It will need to set bond assumptions. 
- It will need to set expense assumptions. 
- It will need to set new business volume assumptions to spread the development costs.
- It should consider whether data is available to set these assumptions.
- It should consider whether it could meet the required profit margin / return on capital. 
- It should check the sensitivity of profit 
- by stress testing its assumptions.
- It should also cover the cost of offering the guaranteed return of premium on death
- which may need to be modelled stochastically 
- and will depend on the mortality of the target market.
- It will need to allow for the cost of capital, eg to meet reserving requirements. 
- It will need to know the cost of the call option 
- and the yield on the bond used to provide the return on premium guarantee.
- All charges received at the outset, so there is no opportunity to increase them if costs increase.
- It is important to monitor sales volumes against the availability of options 
- and reprice if option prices change.

**Competition** 
- The company should consider the charges set by competitors 
- for similar products offered to young professionals.

**Marketing** 
- The company should consider the marketability of the product when setting charges. 
- The company should consider the likely income of the target market.
- Young professionals may have currently low disposable income, but potential for high growth in the future.
- So the company may want a lower charge to attract these people to the business (so the contract may be seen as a loss leader).
- The company then hopes to sell them other products in the long term
- The possibility of cross-subsidies should be considered.
- The company may consider offering this product at a low margin 
- using cross-subsidies with another product
- particularly if it is considered beneficial to get high-value policyholders on board early in their careers.

**Other**
- It should consider regulatory requirements.
- It should consider tax treatment of the product.
- It should consider whether to reinsure the guaranteed return of premium on death 
- and incorporate the costs associated with this in the charge.

<u><b>Q3(iii): Describe the major risks faced by the company as a result of offering this product.</b></u>
**Market risk – call option** 
- The company is exposed to risk relating to derivatives.
- The success of the product depends largely on securing an appropriate call option that matches the liabilities.
- There is a risk that the terms available may change between product pricing and the purchase of the option;
- e.g. the price of the call option could increase;
- which could mean that charges are lower than expected (if they must pay more for the derivative);
- which could reduce profitability;
- or the appropriate maturity date is no longer available.

**Market risk – fixed-interest bond** 
- The company is exposed to interest rate risk.
- If interest rates fall (between pricing and sale of the product), then a higher proportion of the premium may need to be invested in fixed-interest bonds to meet the maturity guarantee;
- which leaves less to take as charges;
- and leaves less to invest in the derivative; 
- making it difficult to meet the index portion of the payout.

**Mortality risk** 
- More deaths than expected will increase the cost of the guaranteed return of premium. 
- The bonds are chosen to meet the premium at maturity;
- so will almost certainly be worth less than premium on death before maturity.
- Given target market, this risk is likely to be low 

**New business mix and volume**
- The mix of new business may be different from that assumed in pricing (e.g. the premium size). 
- There may be lower volumes of new business than expected.
- Changing consumer demand 
- may lead to changes in new business mix and volume.
- New business volumes may be low if there is more competition than expected. 
- Competitors may offer similar products with lower charges or higher guarantees.
- Other products may prove to be more appealing to young professionals (e.g. unit-linked savings products with low/no penalties on surrender).
- High new business volumes is also a risk as there might not be enough call options available to match the guarantee (without moving the price).

**Expense risk** 
- There is a risk of expenses being higher than expected in the pricing, 
- e.g. due to higher than expected inflation.
- The company cannot increase charges easily if expenses are high.

**Credit risk** 
- The company is exposed to the risk of failure of a counterparty;
- arising from the bond issuer;
- and the derivative issuer.

**Operational risk**
- The company is exposed to operational risk (relating to the failure of internal processes, people and systems, and external events).
- For example, it risks accusations of mis-selling if the young investors do not fully understand the risks.
- There is a risk that the models used by the company are inadequate.
- For example, they may incorrectly model the mortality risk when calculating the cost of the guaranteed death payment.
- Other examples of operational risk include:
	- incorrectly pricing the guarantees using inadequate data
	- policyholder literature being unclear on the risks poor service causing reputational risk / loss of business
	- conduct risk (company behaviour that results in poor outcomes for customers)
	- systems unable to cope with the complexity of the product, e.g. averaging the growth in the index.

**Reputational risk**
- Poor outcomes for policyholders could lead to reputational risk.
- If the index rises significantly, receiving only the value of the bonds on surrender could be perceived as unreasonable.
- Customers may be dissatisfied with the maturity value 
- if the index has been low throughout the term but rises sharply before maturity, customers may expect a higher payout to reflect final value of the index 
- rather than the average growth over the term which may be much lower.
- Death benefit may be much lower than current index value
- which could be poorly perceived.

**Other risks**
- There may be regulatory risks (e.g. regulator concerned about the complexity of the product).
- The way in which the product is taxed may change in the future. 

<u><b>Q3(iv): Suggest controls that the company might put in place to manage the risks.</b></u>
**Market risk – call option** 
- Ensure time period between derivative agreement and pricing/selling is as short as possible.
- May need to cap sales volumes to the level of derivative assets available.

**Market risk – fixed-interest bond**
- Continual monitoring of interest rates.
- May need to consider pausing sales if interest rates fall too low. 

**Mortality risk**
- Clearly define the target market
- that is, what do they mean by “young professional”
- Set limits for extent of mortality risk they will take on (e.g. when designing the size of the death benefit).

**New business mix and volume**
- Perform regular review of competitive landscape.
- Review pricing regularly.
- Monitor new business levels and mix of business.
- Regular market research to understand consumer needs.

**Expense risk**
- Manage expenses through a cost cutting regime. 
- Consider outsourcing customer services.

 **Counterparty risk**
- Limit exposure to individual counterparties.
- Set investment limits in particular derivatives or bonds.
- Perform due diligence on counterparties.
- Consider credit rating of counterparty.
- Hold collateral.

**Operational risk**
- Ensure thorough documentation of modelling process. 
- Changes to methodology should be tested and reviewed. 
- Put checks in place to ensure data quality.
- Work closely with the legal department to ensure contracts are clear to customers. 
- Ensure any advice offered meets regulatory standards.
- Carry out regular customer satisfaction surveys. 
- Train staff on the features of the new product, including sales channel and modelling team. 

**Reputational risk** 
- Ensure product literature is clear and easily understood by the target market. 
- Set up processes for clear communication with customers.
- Provide transparent explanations of the payout on surrender and death.
- Provide a transparent explanation for the application of the average return on the index in the maturity value calculation.

**Other**
- Regular monitoring of regulatory developments.
- Have a well-documented Risk Appetite Framework.
