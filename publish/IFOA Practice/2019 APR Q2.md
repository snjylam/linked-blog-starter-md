<u><b>Q2(i): Justify why the company has adopted different investment strategies for F1 and F2</b></u>.
- The NTVOG is invested in less risky assets. So, the company may be aiming to de-risk (i.e. reduce the risk in) its with profits fund. 
- When asset shares reduce due to equity market falls;
- the NTVOG will correspondingly increase;
- due to a higher time value cost of guarantees biting;
- and due to needing to smooth upwards;
- but the assets backing the NTVOG do not reduce in the same way. 
- Fixed interest assets therefore provide better matching of the NTVOG liability than equities. 
- The NTVOG is closely linked to the fixed guaranteed benefit payable;
- whereas the asset shares ignore it;
- as does the estate;
- So it is more appropriate to focus on fixed interest investments for the NTVOG.
- By taking less risk in the assets backing the NTVOG, the company may consider that it can invest the asset shares in riskier assets;
- potentially generating higher overall returns;
- and so allowing it to offer higher bonus rates;
- to help retain existing policyholders. 
- The approach to investment strategy will be set out in the PPFM;
- also, it may have been set out in past communications to policyholders;
- thus, setting the reasonable expectations of policyholders.

<u><b>Q2(ii): Comment on what is wrong with the above approach.</b></u>
- The return should be calculated by solving 90,000 × (1+i) + cashflows × (1+i/2).
- This means that the investment return rate should be deducted from the 84,980 in the equation since it includes the investment return earned (and is otherwise double-counted).
- This means that the 10.836% rate is understated.
- The approach is simplistic in that it divides by 2 as opposed to using compounding.
- This could make a significant difference when dealing with large numbers.
- The use of the averaging of the estate at the beginning and end of the year when allocating the return to the estate means that the NTVOG is assumed to change half way through the year when we are told that it changes at the end of the year.
- This impacts both the rate calculated
- and the allocation of the rate between asset shares and the estate. 
- The approach taken does not take account of which cashflows are applicable to the asset shares and which apply to the estate.

<u><b>Q2(vii): Set out the points the WPA should cover in the report.</b></u>
**Death claims**
- Death claims are 60% higher than asset shares;
- and as such more is being paid out on death than it is on maturity or surrender. 
- This is likely to be due either to the guarantees on death biting;
-  or to the approach taken by the company to allocate bonuses;
- for example the absolute terminal bonuses may be the same as for maturities of the same duration, which are likely to have higher asset shares (relatively). 
- This in itself is not necessarily a cause for concern, as death claims are not material;
- but the WPA may consider whether it is worth investigating the fairness between;
- death claims and maturities;
- and potentially reducing the death benefits.

**Maturity payouts**
- Even though maturity payouts have been increased due to the distribution of the estate;
- the amount paid out remains lower than the asset shares;
- and lower than that being paid out on surrender.
- Since smoothed asset shares are currently lower than unsmoothed;
- this is unlikely to be seen as fair by policyholders who do not surrender.
- It also seems contradictory to be distributing the estate and increasing it (through smoothing costs) at the same time. 
- The WPA is likely to want to take action on this aspect as soon as possible. 
- For example changing its smoothing level/approach;
- or the estate distribution rate/approach.

**Surrenders**
- Surrendering policyholders receive their unsmoothed asset shares with no estate distribution.
- This in isolation may appear fair;
- but not when compared to the maturing policyholders. 
- There were no surrender profits made which needed to be allocated to asset shares or the estate;
- and as such this does not need to be considered. 
- But those who surrender may feel that they are being unfairly treated at times when unsmoothed asset shares are lower than smoothed asset shares;
- especially early on. 
- So the WPA may reconsider having different smoothing approaches applied to maturities and surrenders.

**Expenses**
- Expenses appear very high;
- at around 7% of asset share. 
- This could be for the following reasons.
- The numbers of policies is reducing but the overheads remain the same. 
- If this is the case, the WPA should take action to reverse this;
- such as reducing expenses through efficiency savings;
- merging with another company;
- arranging an expense deal with the shareholders;
- outsourcing.
- There could be one-off expenses in the year;
- e.g. a new IT system.
- if this is the case, the WPA should consider whether some of these are attributable to the estate;
- or whether they should be spread over a number of years. 
- Bearing in mind the fact that new business sales have dropped, any new development costs should be scrutinised to ensure they give fair value to the policyholders.
- The WPA should also consider how the expenses have been allocated across different groups of policyholders;
- and should ensure they are each fairly charged. 

**Shareholder transfers**
- Shareholder transfers appear to be quite high;
- being nearly 4% of total claims.
- This could suggest that regular bonus allocated to in-force business is relatively high.
- So a higher weighting towards terminal bonus may be preferred.

**Unexplained**
1. The unexplained change in the analysis of change in asset shares is quite high;
2. and it is resulting in lower asset shares.
3. So this would need to be better understood, in case there are issues that need to be addressed.

**Estate**
1. A small part of the estate has been distributed;
2. but the estate has grown due to the returns made;
3. and also due to smoothing profits.
4. The estate is a large percentage of the overall assets, relative to asset share.
5. This raises a concern of the risk of a tontine effect occurring.
6. The WPA may therefore take steps to manage this;
7. e.g. by increasing the pace of the distribution. 

**Policy volumes**
- Policy volumes appear to have reduced
- by in excess of 15% (based on the asset shares exiting). 
- The WPA should consider whether new business is likely to increase in the future.
- If not, then there is a strong chance of a tontine effect occurring
- and there being an inequitable allocation of the estate across generations of policyholders.
- The WPA therefore may need to take steps to manage this 
- e.g. by increasing the pace of the estate distribution approach 
- or using a special one-off bonus.
- Consideration should be given to closing the fund to new business and/or producing a run-off plan.

**Investment income**
- Investment income on the estate is high.
- However, it is not necessarily the case that this will continue. 
- The WPA may wish to consider whether the estate should be invested in less volatile assets.
- and whether it should improve the matching of assets and liabilities 
- e.g. through the use of derivatives.

**General considerations**
- Generally, the WPA may need to improve or increase communications to policyholders.

<u><b>Q2(viii): Evaluate suggestion to distribute estate.</b></u>
**Advantages**
- The suggestion means that as asset shares reduce with policies exiting, the estate would be distributed consistently. 
- The suggestion ensures that for a high estate, action is taken
- whilst ensuring judgement can be used where the estate falls within a band.
- This will help to prevent a tontine effect occurring
- and therefore unfairness between generations of policyholders. 

**Disadvantages**
- The suggestion takes no account of the fact that the company also has to hold the NTVOG;
- and also under Solvency II, the risk margin;
- and SCR. 
- So either x and y have to be so large as to ensure there is sufficient capital;
- meaning there is little chance of distributing the estate;
- or there is a danger that the company distributes too much;
- meaning the company cannot cover its capital requirements. 
- The estate is invested partly in risky assets, making the estate volatile;
- even though it moves in line with asset shares, it is likely that if equities fell, there would not be adequate assets to cover the capital requirements;
- or, if equities rose, then too much might be distributed. 
- The suggestion does not allow for the possibility of changes in capital requirements due to risk management.
- As expenses are high, the risk margin in respect of expenses could be high.
- This may be something that is addressed at some stage, resulting in a step change in the technical provisions. 
- De-risking the assets backing the estate, or other hedging strategies could reduce the SCR, freeing up capital. 
- The proposal takes no account of the possibility of new business increasing which would require capital.

**Practicalities**
- From a practical perspective, x% and y% will be difficult to set 
- It would need to be agreed whether they would be fixed or could be changed if conditions vary materially;
- or over time;
- and how that decision to change would be governed.
- There may need to be more clarity or boundaries given around the "some of the estate" rule.
- Overall it is unlikely that this would be a suitable approach to take in its current form.
- However, the suggestion to make the estate distribution more formal is an appropriate suggestion.
- It is easy to explain and calculate. 
- If distributions are through final bonus, and so distributions could be clawed back, then it might be acceptable. 
- But it does not give any indication about how the estate is to be distributed
-  specifically, how it would be distributed between cohorts of policyholders. 
-  There is room, therefore, for expert judgement.
