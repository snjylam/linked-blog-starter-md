<u><b>Q3(iv): Discuss if the approach used to determine the asset share for UWP B units is appropriate.</b></u>
**Reasons for approach being appropriate'**
- This approach will mean that the charges applied to UWP B units will be the same as the unit-linked funds offered under Product B.
- This makes disclosing the charges to customers much easier;
- making the with-profits fund more transparent to customers;
- which may help the sales process.
- It is a much easier asset share calculation than the traditional asset share calculation making it less prone to error;
- uses actual returns which is appropriate.

**'Reasons against approach being appropriate' charges v costs**
- The main issue is how well the 0.9% of assets relates to the amounts actually incurred by the WP fund.
- It is possible that the shareholder has come to an agreement that the NPF will charge the WPF 0.9% and all charges associated with UWP B will be charged to the NPF'
- thus the charges to asset share equal the charges incurred by the WPF
- but the fund is described as 90:10;
- so if the WPF is charged the underlying costs;
- together with shareholder transfers;
- then there will be a mismatch between costs and charges.

**Fairness between different WP policyholders**
- which will emerge in the estate;
- so may impact other customers in the WPF.
- This conflict of interest between different groups of policyholders, for example, between UWP A and UWP B.
- The extent of this conflict will depend on whether the estate is being distributed or not.
- The fairness of this potential or actual conflict would have to have been considered at the time this approach was adopted
- by the appropriate governance, for example consulting with the With-Profits Actuary and review by the With-Profits Committee (or equivalent);
- and should be disclosed in appropriate documentation, for example PPFM.
- Takes no account of the actual tax paid.
- Does not consider the cost of guarantees
