**NP annuities** 
- Annuities will use the GMM/BBA
- as this is relevant for traditional assurance product.
- No underlying pool of assets

**Term Assurance**
- TA will also use the GMM/BBA;
- for the same reasons as the annuities.
- No underlying pool of assets.

**Unit linked endowment assurance**
- UL products use the VFA method;
- as the cashflows vary in line with the UL assets.

<u><b>Q3(ii): Suggest possible reasons for the differences between the Solvency II BEL and IFRS 17 BEL at YE24 for the two product types.</b></u>
**Expenses** 
- Expenses are lower for both products under IFRS17 compared to Sol II.
- Suggests some expenses are classified differently between Sol II and IFRS17;
- with less expenses classified as maintenance and hence reserved for under IFRS17;
- may be due to the attributable vs non-attributable definition under IFRS17
- Impact on annuities is less than for TA for expenses change. Ie as a % of opening reserves.
- Could be due to duration of book ;
- i.e. annuities have a lower remaining duration than TA;
- or expense assumptions may be impacted more under IFRS17 for TA than for annuities.

**RDR**
- RDR movement is a reduction in reserves;
- which suggests that the RDR is higher under IFRS17 than Sol II.
- Could be due to differences in the RFR;
- as SII RFR is prescribed while IFRS17 companies can use own backing assets.
- Or differences in the ILP.
- Under Sol II annuities might have an MA;
- which is similar to ILP;
- but TA don't have a concept of MA;
- but may have a VA.
- Whereas under IFRS17 they may have an ILP on both products;
- so higher impact for TA.
- Demographic assumptions are unchanged;
- and inflation is unchanged;
- which is expected as both reporting regimes assume best estimate assumptions;
- so would be odd to have different views of best estimate under the two regimes.

<u><b>Q3(iii): Discuss how these changes in experience and assumptions will impact the elements of the actuarial results under Solvency II and IFRS 17, respectively.</b></u>
**Solvency II**
- **Assumption changes will impact the BEL**;
- for both mortality and longevity (mortality improvements);
- and changes to the BEL will impact the RM.
- SCR... mortality will change;
- SCR.. Longevity will change;
- second order SCR due to duration changes, for other risks;
- as the RM relates to the non-hedgeable risks'
- which mortality and longevity are.
- **Actual experience will impact the assets**;
- as claims will reduce the assets;
- due to payments to policyholders;
- and liabilities as policies in force reduce.
- **Mortality will impact both assets and liabilities (experience and assumption change)**;
- but unlikely to be an equal impact.
- Whereas longevity will impact liabilities more as reflects views of the future rather than past experience
- So profit will be impacted by assumption changes.

**IFRS17**
- **Assumption changes will impact BEL**;
- and Risk Adjustment;
- similar to Sol II.
- Actual experience will impact assets and BEL/RA similarly too.
- Experience variance will feed through the P&L.
- **Assumption changes will also impact CSM**;
- as the CSM is the expected future profit/unearned profit;
- which will offset BEL movement.
- May recognise a loss component if CSM becomes neg (i.e. zeroised).
- **Similarly, the CSM will be impacted by actual experience vs expected**;
- so profit will be less impacted;
- as the assumption change will impact both BEL/RA and CSM.
- This is where IFRS17 differs to Sol II;
- as profit is less sensitive to assumption changes.

<u><b>Q3(iv): Discuss suitable refinements that could be made to the approach to address the actuary’s concerns, without running the full model.</b></u>
**Number of policies**
- Current proposal does not allow for actual offs over the period.
- Only allows for expected offs;
- based on prior year end assumptions;
- which are likely to differ from actual.
- So could look at actual claims vs expected claims over the period;
- and scaling the reserving cashflows to allow for any material variance.
- Or speak to customer services to understand if there were any odd movement over the period;
- and/or large one-off claims;
- and allow for these manually.
- Or use internal MI on experience if it exists.

**Assumption changes**
- Current proposal does not allow for assumption changes over the HY.
- If material assumption changes, then could allow for these manually;
- either approximatively;
- or via an estimate from the assumptions setting team;
- or using sensitivities;
- and scaling the sensitivity stress for the actual assumption change.

**Market movements**
- Current proposal does not allow for market movements on economic assumptions;
- e.g. RFR changes, inflation, ILP.
- If material changes then could allow for manually using sensitives;
- but this may not work if the yield curve twisted rather than shifted.

**New business**
- Current proposal does not allow for new business (if relevant).
- Allow for NB if material;
- using MI;
- or scaling from a prior period.
- Can adjust expected expenses for actual expenses from finance results.
- Could consult auditors in advance to see if approach is materially suitable.
- Use the SII impacts to estimate the IFRS.

<u><b>Q3(v): Discuss why the actuary’s proposed approach may not be appropriate for the non-profit business.</b></u>
- Depends how well matched the assets and liabilities are;
- unit linked assets and liabilities are well matched;
- so the proposed approach makes sense for UL
- Whereas for NP products the A and L aren't as closely matched;
- so the A and L don't move in line as much.
- Also, the UL products use the VFA model.
- So the CSM is unlocked for changes in the BEL due to the RDR changes.
- Whereas the CSM for NP products is not unlocked for RDR changes.
- New Business often has small unit reserve so using BEL estimate will give close to nil NB BEL which could be materially wrong.
