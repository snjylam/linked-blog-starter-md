<u><b>Q2(i): State the three lines of defence a company should have as part of its enterprise risk management.</b></u>
**Three lines of defence**
- First line is business operations;
- where a well established control environment is embedded in day-to-day operation.
- Second line is oversight function (e.g. standalone risk function);
- which is responsible for production, implementation, and monitoring of risk management processes.
- Third line is independent assurance providers (e.g. internal and external audit);
- which evaluate and challenge the company's risk management processes.

<u><b>Q2(ii): State six possible example outcomes of treating customers fairly.</b></u>
**FCA six defined consumer outcomes**
- Consumers can be confident dealing with firms where TCF is central to corporate culture. 
- Products sold must meet the needs of consumer groups and are targeted accordingly.
- Consumers are provided with clear information and are kept appropriately informed.
- Advice given to consumers should be suitable and takes account of their circumstances.
- Products performs and services is of an acceptable standard as firms have led consumers to expect.
- Consumers do not face unreasonable barriers to switch products/provider, submit a claim/complaint.

<u><b>Q2(iii): Discuss how Company A can risk of not meeting each of the outcomes for policyholders who accept Company A's offer.</b></u>
**Approaches to achieve consumer outcomes**
- Co A needs to consider what level of lump sum would be considered fair. 
- Co A needs to ensure customer data is suitably protected. 
- Co A needs to ensure they target the right groups of policyholders and do not put their dependents at financial risk. 
- For example, if the product is to provide life cover for a mortgage, then the settlements firm should ensure dependents have means to pay the mortgage.
- Co A needs to ensure literature provided clearly states that the death benefit is no longer received by their beneficiaries and the potential implications of this.
- There are no ‘after sale’ implications. 
- Co A needs to ensure customers are aware of the risks, and they may require provision to be made for dependents elsewhere.
- For example, Co A may require some money to be put in trust for dependents;
- or may require dependents to agree to the sale;
- or may limit the target market to only policyholder with no dependents.
- Co A may recommend policyholders seek financial advice.
- Co A needs to ensure it avoids using pressurising sales techniques. 
- Co A needs to ensure timely payment to the policyholder.
- Co A needs to allow a cooling off period.
- Co A needs to have a process in place for complaints

<u><b>Q2(iv): Discuss the factors Company A needs to consider when determining the level of lump sum to offer.</b></u>
**Treat customers fairly**
- The company needs to ensure the lump sum provided is considered fair. 
- The company needs to suitably protect customer data. 
- The company needs to ensure it is targeting the right groups of policyholders and not put dependents at financial risk. 
- For example, if the life cover was to repay a mortgage, the life settlement firm should ensure dependents have the means to repay the mortgage.
- The company could instead target policyholders with no dependents.
- The company should make it clear in literature that the death benefit is no longer paid.
- The company should ensure customers are aware of the implications of not receiving a benefit on death; 
- and that they may require to make sufficient provision elsewhere. 
- The company should ensure it does not put customers under pressure to sell the policy.
- The company should recommend policyholders seek financial advice.
- The company could require dependents to agree to the sale.
- The company needs to ensure payments are made in a timely manner. 
- The company should apply a cooling-off period. 
- The company should set up a customer services team to handle complaints.
- The company should ensure no future premiums are requested from the policyholder once company has paid the lump sum.

<u><b>Q2(v): Discuss the other ongoing risks faced by Company A once policyholders have accepted the offer.</b></u>
**Mortality risk**
- There is a risk that policyholders live longer than expected;
- so the firm is paying premiums for longer;
- and receiving the death benefit later than expected. 
- Equally people may die sooner than expected leading to fairness questions from dependent(s).
- There is a risk that underwriting is not performed adequately;
- or exaggeration by customers on their health issues to get a higher lump sum;
- e.g. smoker status, etc. 
- There is a risk of anti-selection as policyholders with healthier lives are more likely to take the lump sum.
- There is a risk that the company lacks relevant experience data for pricing.

**Liquidity risk**
- There is liquidity risk when initially setting up;
- as Company A will need to pay the premiums;
- and pay out lump sums but not receiving the sum assured for a long time.
- There is a risk that the mix of expected payments vs premiums is wrong;
- or volumes are not as planned. 
- There is a risk of delays in receiving payment from life insurance company on death of life assured. 
- There is risk of insolvency of insurance company so DB not paid.

**Operational risk**
- There is a risk Company A does not know the policyholder has died;
- as dependents don’t have any vested interest to inform Company A;
- which could result in delays in receiving death benefit from insurance company;
- and achieve lower returns than expected on investments.
- There is a risk the company gives the wrong quote to the policyholder.
- There is a risk of administration system errors;
- e.g. causing the company to fails to pay the correct premium to the insurance company.
- There is a risk the company's processes may fail to ensure that the life insurance company records are correctly endorsed so that Company A is the beneficiary. 
- There is a risk of fraud.

**Model risk**
- There is a risk that the company has an inadequate model for pricing;
- or a correct model was used but incorrectly specified.
- There is a risk that the life insurance company doesn’t pay on death due to an invalidating clause, e.g. suicide etc. 
- There is a risk of complaints from dependents when they don’t receive the death benefit; 
- leading to reputational risk. 

**Conduct risk** 
- There is a risk that the material presented to the policyholder may mis-represent the offer; 
- which may have misled policyholders into entering the arrangement. 
- There is a risk that the policyholder does not having the appropriate information to make a well-informed decision.
