<u><b>Q3(iv): Discuss how both assumption changes would impact the reserves (present value of cash flows), the CSM and profit for the product, and how the impacts would be determined.</b></u>
- Strengthening the mortality assumption will increase the value placed on the BEL;
- which will reduce the surplus;
- as assets won't change.
- The CSM will reduce as future profits are expected to be lower;
- as the BEL has increased.
- Overall the mortality assumption change will have minimal impact on profit as the BEL impact will be offset by the CSM change
- The CSM is calculated using the GMM which means it is on a locked in interest rate;
- such that changes in financial assumptions don't impact the CSM;
- but changes in expected future experience do impact the CSM.
- So a mortality assumption change would change the CSM.
- To make the change the BEL will be recalculated on the CSM locked in rate in order to;
- perform the mortality assumption change on the locked basis;
- which will then impact the CSM
- The BEL would then be moved back to the unlocked basis before adjusting for any financial assumption changes which only impacts the BEL not the CSM.
- An increase in the discount rate will decrease the liabilities but it is likely that the assets will have decreased too;
- so the impact on surplus will depend on the degree of matching;
- and the approach taken to derive the discount rate.
- As the CSM is on a locked in interest rate the CSM won't change which means the change in liabs will impact profit.
- If the movement in BEL>CSM there is a loss component.

<u><b>Q3(vi): Discuss why the IFRS17 risk discount rate has increased by more than the Solvency II risk-free rate.</b></u>
- The bottom up IFRS17 RDR comprises of a risk free rate;
- and an illiquidity premium.
- Setting the RDR considers the assets held by the firm;
- whereas the SII rate is a risk free rate;
- plus the ability to apply a VA or MA with approval.
- The risk free rate is likely to be fairly consistent between SII and IFRS17 but the addition of the ILP is likely to be the driver of the differences;
- this reflects the spreads on the corp bonds and gilts held;
- and can be derived in different way.
- IFRS17 ILP driven by liquidity characteristics of liabilities, but SII it is characteristics of assets.
