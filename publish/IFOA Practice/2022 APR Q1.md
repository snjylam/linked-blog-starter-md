<u><b>Q1(i): Suggest reasons why the company might decide not to enter the group life market.</b></u>
**Meeting customer needs**
- The target market for group life is different to that of individual term assurance. 
- The target market for group life products are employers and there may be a lack of demand;
- e.g. due to a recession leading to employers cutting back on employee benefits. 

**Profit / Risk / Capital** 
- The company may feel that the group product does not meet the company's profit criterion.
- The company may feel that it does not have the necessary experience managing group products;
- and as such lacks the necessary data to price the product.
- The development costs may be too high. 
- The mix of lives in group products is different than the mix of individual term assurance policyholders. 
- The company may feel it does not have sufficient mortality experience to price the product,
- The company may feel it does not fit well with the company's risk appetite;
- notably concentration risk;
- e.g. the risk of mass claims due to catastrophe at place of work. 
- Reinsurance arrangements for group products are different to that of individual term assurance.
- The company may not have an existing reinsurer that deals with group products;
- or it may be too expensive to do so.
- The capital requirements of the product may be too high;
- and the company may believe it can earn a higher return using other strategies. 

**Distribution / Administration / Competition**
- The company's existing distribution channels may be inappropriate due to the different target market. 
- The company may face significant costs to set up a new channel. 
- The company may need to make updates to administration systems to administer group products;
- to cope with annually reviewable premiums, for example. 
- The company may feel the group business is too competitive; 
- and the company may feel it would be unable to cover fixed expenses even with competitive pricing.

**Regulation / Taxation**
- The company may feel the regulatory requirements surrounding sale of group business is too restrictive. 
- The taxation of this product might be different.

<u><b>Q1(iii): Discuss why the company may hold more capital than that indicated by its assessment of economic capital requirements.</b></u>
**Risk capital**
- It may have uncertainty around the assumptions used in the economic assessment so is holding additional capital to cover the uncertainty.
- The company may not trust the output from the model used to calculate the economic capital.
- It may feel that the model does not reflect all the risks.

**Regulatory capital vs Economic capital**
- The regulatory capital assessment may show a higher capital requirement.
- This may be as a result of restrictions in the methodology used
- e.g. the extent to which future premiums are valued / diversification benefits are allowed for.
- The regulatory liabilities may be on a best estimate basis with the prudence being reflected in the capital requirement
- e.g. holding enough capital to withstand a 1 in 200 year event. 
- Whereas the economic capital assessment may be more realistic.
- The company may be subject to a capital add-on under a regulatory assessment.
- The economic capital assessment is likely to be a basis for the company’s risk assessment framework;
- and this could have generated a range of possible capital requirements, with the company choosing to hold capital at the higher end of the range.

**Rating agency capital** 
- If the rating agency capital requirement is higher, the company may have decided to hold this
- to achieve a particular credit rating.
- The company may want to raise additional capital;
- and a healthy capital position will enable it to access cheaper capital. 

**Working capital**
- The additional working capital might be needed to support rapid new business growth in excess of its business plan projections.
- It may want to be able to adopt new technologies, e.g. to help with its sales process. 
- The company may be looking to acquire another life insurer.

**Stakeholders**
- It is a way of giving confidence to external stakeholders.
- However, the company wouldn’t want to hold too much capital;
- as this may also have a negative impact; appearing it has no alternative investments.
- The capital position may better match competitors’ positions.

<u><b>Q1(v): Describe the main risks that would impact the level of the company's SCR.</b></u>
 **Interest rate (market) risk**
 - There is a risk of mismatch between its assets and liabilities. 
 - This risk is unlikely to be material if government bonds are held as closely-matched assets.
 - If interest rates change then the Solvency II discount rate should change so the impact on assets less liabilities is minimised. 

**Mortality risk**
- There is a risk that there are higher than expected death claims under the term assurance product;
- which may be due to any inadequacies in pricing or underwriting;
- and is problematic as there are no other products to diversify this risk.
- The extent of mortality risk will depend on any reinsurance arrangements in place. 

**Lapse risk**
- There is a risk of higher than expected lapses early on in the product term, resulting in initial expenses not being recouped.
- There is a risk of selective lapsing.

**Expense risk**
- There is a risk of under-estimating expenses and is particularly significant due to the expense loadings comprising a high proportion of the small regular premiums.
- There is also a risk of higher than expected inflation.
- The extent of expense risk will depend on the amount of outsourcing in place.

**Credit / counterparty risk**
- There is credit risk which is the risk of higher than expected defaults on the corporate bonds used to match the liabilities. 
- There is counterparty risk which is the risk of defaults of any reinsurance arrangements covering the term assurance products.

**Operational risk**
- There is risk of potential mis-selling of a product,

**Catastrophe risk**
- There is catastrophe risk, e.g. where the company experiences a large number of claims, due to a pandemic.
- The extent of catastrophe risk will depend on how geographically concentrated the target market is.
