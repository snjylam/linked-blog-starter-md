<u><b>Q1(i): Explain the reasons for producing each item, as outlined above, in the analysis of surplus.</b></u>
**Re-run start year model**
- This confirms starting position equals reported numbers.
- Need to allow for opening adjustments, i.e. model changes.

**Run model with new non-economic assumptions**
- This shows the impact of changing assumptions.
- For example, to show sensitivity of results to strengthening the mortality improvement assumption.

**Roll model forward with actual investment and year-end discount rate** 
- This provided the total economic variance;
- including any mismatching profit / loss.
- This will show the appropriateness of the credit default adjustment;
- or in the matching approach being taken.

**Add new business written**
- This shows the impact of new business written.

**Adjust for differences between actual and modelled** 
- This shows impact of actual vs expected non-economic experience.
- A release of the margins would be expected;
- leading to small profit.
- If difference is big there may be an error in assumption setting;
- which may lead to a change in assumption.

**Final adjustments**
- This shows the impact of surplus due to capital injections.
- This shows the impact of profits due to tax changes or differences. 

**Unexplained**
- Any unexplained item should be within tolerance.
- If difference is big then there may be an error in the calculations.

**General**
- These items may be compared against previous years to identify trends.
- The order of these items would need to be kept the same for analysis to be meaningful.
- Any of the steps may be a disclosure requirement.

<u><b>Q1(ii): Suggest reasons why it may not be possible to get an accurate figure for each of the items.</b></u>
**Model rolled forward with actual investment returns**
- The investment returns may be inaccurate.
- The assets at year-end may be different from assets rolled forward.
- The asset breakdown may not be granular enough.
- The assets may have changed rating which may not be reflected in model.
- The model may not know which assets have been sold or bought over the period.
- he model may not be able to value complex assets accurately.
- The model may not produce accurate timing of cashflows.
- The model may not capture asset defaults.
- The investment fees may be too complex to model.

**New business written**
- The model points could be used instead of policy by policy run.
- There may be delays in investing new business premiums which are not captured by model.
- There may be delays in reporting new business.
- There may be lapses in the cooling-off period.

**Actual to modelled longevity**
- The actual deaths may be an adjustment to mortality tables so are subject to averaging, rather than using data on actual deaths from a movements file.
- The model may not capture actual deaths so there may be a skewed experience (e.g. average death values are higher than the average values overall).
- The model may approximate timing of deaths so actual annuities paid in period may not be captured in full.

**Actual to modelled expenses**
- The modelled total per policy expenses may be inaccurate due to the policy measure used.
- Any averaging of expenses per policy could use different in-force policies than those used in the model.

<u><b>Q1(vi): Discuss how a move to IFRS17 might impact liabilities and future accounting profit from the annuity business.</u></b>
**IFRS 17 vs IFRS 4 liabilities**
- The General Measurement Model would be used.
- The IFRS17 liabilities are on best estimate basis;
- while IFRS4 liabilities are on best estimate plus a margin for prudence;
- so IFRS17 reserve < IFRS4 reserve.
- The IFRS17 discount rates are derived from observable market data
- while IFRS4 discount rates are derived from actual asset yields with a credit default adjustment;
- so IFRS17 > IFRS4 (even with allowance for default since market rates are low).
- Under IFRS17, there is a risk adjustment for non-market risks;
- and if these are higher than the IFRS4 prudence margins
- then IFRS17 liabilities > IFRS4 liabilities.
- Under IFRS17, there is a contractual service margin since the business is profitable
- which offsets day one profit and increase the IFRS17 liabilities.
- but also increases future profit when the CSM is released.
- Under IFRS17, any losses on writing the business is recognised immediately and taken to the profit and loss.

**IFRS 17 vs IFRS 4 profits**
- If products remain profitable, there is no differences due to offsetting profitable and unprofitable contracts.
- The level of CSM will depend on the duration of the contract since the CSM will run off over time.
- The level of CSM will depend on the change in longevity assumption since these go through the CSM.
- However, the CSM does not absorb changes in economic assumptions such as the falling interest rates which would increase liabilities under both approaches.
- For the in-force business the impact on profits will depend on whether the total reserves are higher or lower under IFRS17 or IFRS 4.
- It is likely IFRS17 liabilities are higher so future profits will be higher as these and CSM are released.
- However, if future new business is high, then future profits under IFRS17 may be lower due to the fact that under IFRS17 any day 1 gain will be eliminated via the CSM.
- Although IFRS17 requires unbundling of contacts into insurance and investment components, this is not relevant to annuity business.

<u><b>Q1(vii): Recommend the valuation method that should be used for the unit-linked business under IFRS17.</u></b>
- The Variable Fee Approach should be used;
- since a significant proportion of the cashflows vary in line with the value of a clearly defined pool of assets.
- The discount rate is calculated with reference to the pool of assets.
- There is a risk adjustment to represent non-market risks.
- There is a CSM to eliminate day one profit;
- which offsets the change in BEL and RA due to assumption changes.
- The CSM is unlocked when the discount rate changes.
