<u><b>Q3(i): Describe the company’s liquidity risk associated with unit-linked pension, annuities, and term assurance?</b></u>
**General**
- Liquidity risk is the risk from short-term cashflow mismatches;
- as opposed to the risk from longer-term matching of assets and liabilities.
- There will always be an element of liquidity risk due to any one-off expenditure. 

**Unit-linked pension product**
- For its pension product it arises due to uncertainty if claim payments; 
- e.g. if the policyholder chooses to take a transfer value. 
- However most unit-linked funds are invested in marketable assets;
- so liquidity risk is relatively low;
- but there may be high liquidity risk from any property investment within the unit-linked fund.
- However there may be terms and conditions of the policy that allow the fund to defer paying out immediately. 
- The extent of this risk will depend on the size of any management box. 
- Any choice as to when policyholder decides to take their pension will lead to uncertainty in payouts and liquidity risk.
- The type of investments which the unit-linked funds are invested in will determine the extent of liquidity risk, e.g. long-term capital projects hold a higher liquidity risk.

**Without-profits annuity**
- The benefit payments are guaranteed and so relatively predictable. 
- Therefore they have low liquidity risk. 
- However this depends on how well matched the annuities are; 
- so the cashflows from the assets are paid at the appropriate time to meet the annuity payments. 

**Term assurance**
- The claim payments are less certain than annuities and so face higher liquidity risk
- As this is a new product, the size of the book is relatively small;
- increasing the risk of random fluctuations. 
- The exact extent of liquidity risk will depend on any reinsurance agreements in place.

<u><b>Q3(iii): Assess how the three standard profit criteria may be used by the company to achieve its aim.</b></u>
**Internal rate of return**
- The internal rate of return criterion;
- will ensure that the product delivers a suitable return on the capital employed;
- but may not ensure that the capital is returns quickly;
- which may be a problem if the company has limited capital available.
- In reality, the company will use a combination of the three;
- probably with more weighting to the discounted payback period.

**Discounted payback period**
- The discounted payback period;
- will favour product designs that return capital quickly;
- but may not deliver as favourable returns.

**Net present value of future profits**
- The net present value of future profits approach;
- does not specifically promote capital efficiency;
- but does ensure a reasonable level of profitability

<u><b>Q3(v): Discuss why the company may consider altering the annuity investment strategy so that the assets have a longer duration than the liabilities.</b></u>
- A fall in interest rates may be anticipated;
- so this is a tactical investment decision;
- to profit from;
- rise in assets > rise in liabilities.
- The required capital analysis indicates that additional capital is required if interest rates fall.
- If the company invested assets longer than liabilities and interest rates were to fall, then the value of the assets would rise
- resulting in additional capital if interest rates fall.
- With this strategy, the company would have to hold less required capital against the fall in interest rates risk.
- The company might now be susceptible to a rise in interest rates;
- but this may be mitigated by other product lines.
- The strategy may be appropriate if the overall level of required capital falls as a result;
- but the company is now exposed to a real loss on this business should interest rates rise; 
- compared to the current approach.
